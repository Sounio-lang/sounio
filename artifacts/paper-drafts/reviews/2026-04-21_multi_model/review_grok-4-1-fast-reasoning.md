1. Top 5 concerns, ranked by threat to the main claim.
The central claim--"the sedenion associator norm spikes at ictal onset"--is barely supported by a marginal p=1.23e-2 under iid null on N=24, equivalent to a +22.7% median increase from PRE5 to IC, which is a tiny effect that vanishes in many patients (only 12/24 have positive spike per T5). First threat: the effect is too weak and inconsistent for "spikes," as the cohort median dip is +0.17 (no dip) and spike relies on Fisher-combined p across patients where per-patient p_spike often exceeds 0.05 (Appendix Table shows multiple >0.05). Second: T4 LOO AUC=0.642 [0.513,0.764] has a lower CI hugging 0.5, meaningless for prediction in N=24 (barely "strictly above 0.5" as claimed). Third: three nulls (iid p=0.012, circular p=9e-5, block p=0.03) are cherry-picked robustness without joint correction, inflating evidence; circular shift favors the effect because it poorly models EEG autocorrelation. Fourth: T3 "100/100 sign preservation" uses cohort-median sign on 16-of-23 channels, but with median dip=0.328>0 across draws, contradicting primary T1-refuted dip (p=0.599). Fifth: normalization from interictal II window at -300s leaks seizure context, as "standardised with these statistics and clipped to ±5" artificially stabilizes baseline but not IC.

2. Statistical / pre-registration audit.
BH-FDR family is correctly {T1,T2,T4} at q=0.05, with T2/T4 surviving after ranking (T2 p=0.012 <0.0167 cut, T4 p=0.016<0.033), T1 refuted; supporting {T3,T5} uncorrected as declared. But three nulls for T1/T2 are mishandled as "robustness checks" with separate Fisher p-values ("T2 survives all three"), treated as independent evidence rather than one pre-specified iid primary; protocol claims "iid null is the primary per-pre-registration," yet abstract/Fig3 pool them without caveat, double-dipping. LOO AUC CI [0.513,0.764] is disastrously wide for N=24 (20k boot), lower bound 0.513 is "strictly above 0.5" only by rounding; honest report would flag as non-informative (expected width ~1/sqrt(N) for AUC). Protocol pre-specifies everything, but Hessian pilot "summarised here only to explain why we did not report its numbers" leaks: pilot on n<=7 showed "direction flipped across patients," likely informed abandoning Hessian for associator and choosing sedenion lifting/shifts, violating "frozen before any inferential test"; git SHA unverified without repo.

3. Biomarker construction and algebraic choices.
Sedenion associator norm is arbitrary overkill: lifting maps 16 channels to basis e_0..e_15 as a_t=sum \bar s_{i,t} e_{i-1}, h_t at t-1, x_t at t-2 (why 1/2-sample lags? Arbitrary autocorrelation probe), mean ||[a,h,x]||_2 over T=80. "Minimal Cayley-Dickson where alternative law fails" is ornamental; octonions (dim8) already non-assoc, sedenions add zero-divisors irrelevant here ("grows in magnitude once zero divisors available"). Simpler cubic ||a·(b×c)|| or whitened triple-product tr(s_t s_{t-1}^T s_{t-2}) captures channel cross-phase equally (EEG non-stationarity boosts all trilinear norms at onset); quaternion tensor associator on 4x4 channel groups matches dim16 without Cayley-Dickson pathology. Sedenion load-bearing claim fails: "faithful index of order-sensitivity" ignores that associator vanishes if a,h,x collinear (high-lag autocorr), spikes trivially from decorrelation, not "non-associative geometry" as sold ("how far the recent recurrence structure departs from an octonion subalgebra").

4. Clinical validity and scope.
No, this is not "evidence of a generic pre-ictal/ictal mechanism": CHB-MIT is N=24 pediatric intractable focal epilepsy from single center (Boston Children's, "enriched" per limitations), scalp EEG (noisy, poor localization), first-seizure only ("one ictal recording... first annotated seizure onset"). Paper admits "cannot claim... outside this population" but headlines "spikes at ictal onset in scalp EEG" without qualifying pediatric/focal/scalp limits; windows ±90s around t* ignore variable onsets (annotations unreliable in scalp). Right caveat: "weak signal in toy cohort, requires adult/intracranial/multi-center replication; scalp useless for focal onset." T2 "ictal-onset spike" conflates rhythm desynchronization (known [schindler2007]) with sedenion novelty.

5. Recommendation: reject, with the single most important change required before submission.
Resubmit only after independent replication on adult/intracranial cohort (N>100) with simplified trilinear norm (drop sedenions), pre-registering effect size >50% not p<0.05, and drop all associator/algebraic fluff.
