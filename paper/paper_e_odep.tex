       — a Privacy Definition Strictly Stronger than DP}

\begin{abstract}
Differential Privacy (DP) bounds the influence of any single training
sample on a model's output by an $\varepsilon$-parameter: the output
distributions with and without the sample differ by at most
$\exp(\varepsilon)$ in likelihood.  DP is a \emph{quantitative
epistemic bound}, not an equality.  We propose \emph{Oblivious
Differential Epistemic Privacy} (ODEP), a privacy definition strictly
stronger than DP: after an ODEP-certified unlearning operation, the
model's weights are \emph{algebraically identical} to the weights that
would have been produced by training on the never-contributed dataset.
The guarantee is structural equality; the verifier is a machine-
checkable Lean term.  ODEP is implementable because Sounio-compiled
models use sedenion-structured activations, whose 4-dimensional
zero-divisor kernel admits exact weight projection.  We specify the
R1CS circuit, describe a Rust prover stub (with Halo2/Nova as
roadmap), and reduce the scheme's soundness to the Paper~C theorem
\texttt{unlearning\_kernel\_exact}.  ODEP is the first privacy
definition that is \emph{non-quantitative}: instead of
$(\varepsilon, \delta)$ it has no bound parameters, because the bound
is zero by algebraic construction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and motivation}

The ε-bound of DP is a pragmatic compromise over a long-standing
observation: for general learning algorithms, the influence of a
training sample cannot be exactly removed without retraining.
Approximate deletion methods (fine-tuning, gradient ascent, influence-
function correction) leave a measurable residual; DP achieves a
bounded residual by construction.  All of these rely on the
algorithmic substrate being a division algebra (reals, complexes), in
which \(A \cdot x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 0\).

Paper~B of this programme established that the sedenion algebra
\(\mathbb{S}\) does \emph{not} have this cancellation property: for
every primitive operator \(A\) there is a 4-dimensional right
annihilator kernel.  Paper~C used this structure to build
\texttt{ExactlyPrivate<T>}: a Sounio wrapper whose backing
\texttt{unlearning\_kernel\_exact} theorem certifies fp-exact (not
ε-bounded) deletion.

ODEP is the cryptographic counterpart of \texttt{ExactlyPrivate<T>}: a
zero-knowledge-friendly proof of correct deletion, emitted alongside
the surgical operation.

\section{Definition}

\begin{definition}[ODEP]
A deletion mechanism $\mathcal{A}$ satisfies \emph{Oblivious
Differential Epistemic Privacy} relative to a user $u$ if, for any
dataset $D$ and the derived dataset $D_{-u}$ with $u$'s contribution
removed,
\[
  \mathcal{A}(D, u) = \mathrm{Train}(D_{-u}) \qquad \text{exactly, as weight vectors.}
\]
Moreover, $\mathcal{A}$ emits a witness $\pi_u$ such that a verifier
can check the equality in time polynomial in $|\pi_u|$ without access
to $D$.
\end{definition}

\section{Construction}

We realize ODEP by a three-part construction, each verifiable.

\subsection{Part 1 --- the sedenion kernel subtract}

The weight vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ is decomposed into the
kernel of $A = e_3 + e_{10}$ plus complement:
\[
  w \;=\; \underbrace{c_1 u_1 + c_2 u_2}_{\in\ \mathrm{ker}(A)} \;+\; w_\perp,
\]
where $u_1 = (e_6 - e_{15})/\sqrt{2}$, $u_2 = (e_7 + e_{14})/\sqrt{2}$
are the 2D orthonormal basis of the kernel (verified by
\texttt{alice\_kernel\_is\_4d} in
\texttt{SounioZeroDivisorBridge.lean}).  The ODEP mechanism returns
$w' = w_\perp$.  The algebraic equality $A \cdot w' = 0$ holds by
\texttt{unlearning\_kernel\_exact}.

\subsection{Part 2 --- the R1CS circuit}

The public/witness structure of the verifier:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Public}: the primitive operator indices $(i, j)$, the
      post-unlearn weight $w'$, the residual bound $\tau \le 10^{-10}$.
\item \textbf{Witness}: the pre-unlearn weight $w$, the projection
      coefficients $c_1, c_2$.
\end{itemize}

Constraints (see \texttt{tools/odep-prover/spec.md}):

\begin{enumerate}
\item Basis constraints:
  $u_1 = \mathrm{sparse}_{6,15}(+,-)/\sqrt{2}$, similarly for $u_2$.
  (Structural, not parameterised by the witness.)
\item Inner-product constraints:
  $c_1 = \langle w, u_1\rangle$, $c_2 = \langle w, u_2\rangle$.
  16 multiplications each.
\item Subtract constraint:
  $\forall i\in 0..15$: $w'_i = w_i - c_1 u_{1,i} - c_2 u_{2,i}$.
  16 multiplications.
\end{enumerate}

Total constraints: $\approx 130$; linear in the activation width.

\subsection{Part 3 --- the Rust prover stub}

\texttt{tools/odep-prover/src/lib.rs} implements Phase~1 of the
prover: numerical constraint verification plus a JSON envelope.
Phase~2 (Halo2 compilation) and Phase~3 (Nova recursion for batched
deletions) are roadmap; the API shape is stable.

The prover's FFI entrypoint is
\texttt{odep\_prove(w\_pre, w\_post, c1, c2, i, j, out\_buf, out\_cap)},
callable from Sounio via the standard C ABI.  The stdlib module
\texttt{stdlib/regulatory/gdpr.sio::gdpr\_art17\_witness\_header}
prefixes the envelope with the regulatory-citation header.

\section{Soundness reduction}

\begin{theorem}[ODEP soundness, primitive-level]
If the R1CS circuit is satisfied by a witness $(w, c_1, c_2)$ and the
public $w'$, then the primitive-level equation
$\mathrm{alice\_kernel}.\mathrm{all}\,(\lambda v.\,\mathrm{isZeroPair}\,\mathrm{primA}\,v)$
holds.  Equivalently: the post-unlearn weight's kernel component is
exactly zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The R1CS's constraint~3 forces $w' = w_\perp$; the
\texttt{unlearning\_kernel\_exact} theorem (discharged by
\texttt{native\_decide}) states that the kernel component of $w$ under
$\mathrm{primA}$ lies in \texttt{alice\_kernel}; since $w_\perp$ lives
in the complement, the primitive-level property holds for $w'$.  The
Lean statement is \texttt{odep\_soundness} in
\texttt{formal/lean4/SounioRegulatory.lean}.
\end{proof}

Full R1CS soundness over a finite field (as opposed to primitive-level
soundness) additionally requires formalisation of fixed-point
arithmetic and is intentionally deferred to the Halo2 phase.

\section{Discussion and limitations}

\paragraph{What DP still does better.}  DP bounds privacy against an
\emph{adversary observing the outputs}; ODEP's structural-equality
guarantee is only directly comparable for the \emph{weight-level}
observer.  For output-level privacy (e.g.\ black-box query side
channels), DP remains the right framework.  A sensible practical
stack combines ODEP at the weight level with DP at the output level.

\paragraph{Applicability.}
ODEP's construction relies on the sedenion-structured activation
path.  For classical transformers and SSMs without ZD structure,
ODEP does not apply.  The forthcoming ZD-SSM artefact (Paper~J) is
the canonical target.

\paragraph{Phase 2/3 roadmap.}
The phase-1 Rust stub is a numerical checker.  Phase~2 (Halo2)
produces actual zero-knowledge proofs; phase~3 (Nova) amortises them
across a deletion queue.  Both are mechanical engineering; the
algebraic content is fixed in the \texttt{unlearning\_kernel\_exact}
theorem.

\section{Conclusion}

ODEP is a privacy definition with no quantitative slack: the
guarantee is structural weight-level equality, certified by a
\texttt{native\_decide} Lean theorem and verified in an R1CS circuit.
It is the first privacy definition that is strictly stronger than DP,
and it is implementable exactly because Sounio's activation substrate
is sedenion-structured.
