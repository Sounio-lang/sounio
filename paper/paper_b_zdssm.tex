\begin{abstract}
State space models (SSMs) use learned linear transition matrices $A$ to evolve
hidden states. When $A$ is drawn from a division algebra---quaternions, octonions,
or the real or complex numbers---the composition law $\|Ah\|^2 = \|A\|^2 \|h\|^2$
implies that no nonzero state component can be \emph{exactly zeroed}: forgetting
is always approximate, leaving ``ghost beliefs'' that contaminate downstream computation.
We show that sedenions ($\mathbb{S}$), the 16-dimensional Cayley-Dickson algebra,
are the \emph{first and minimal} algebra where this impossibility is broken:
sedenions contain zero divisors (ZD), pairs $(A, h)$ with $A \neq 0$, $h \neq 0$,
yet $A \otimes h = 0$.
We introduce the \emph{ZD-SSM} architecture and formalize (Lean 4) the
168-class structural Bridge Theorem for sedenion zero divisors via
\texttt{native\_decide} over the full multiplication table, connecting it by
a propositional dependency scaffold to Hurwitz's classical
normed-division-algebra boundary.
We report a consistent \textbf{+51.15pp} accuracy advantage across a 10-fold
range of signal-to-contaminant ratios (1.5$\times$--10$\times$), with honest
reporting of the two regimes where the advantage vanishes: when the task signal
already dominates the decay residual (tie) and when the signal falls below the
trained readout's discrimination floor (both models chance-level).
We integrate the guarantee into the Sounio programming language as
$\Forgettable{T}$, enforced by a new \texttt{with ZD} effect, and show that
BPTT gradients flow correctly through sedenion multiplication.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

Structured state space models~\cite{gu2021efficiently,gu2023mamba}
process sequences via $h_{t+1} = A \cdot h_t + B \cdot x_t$.
Standard architectures implement forgetting via eigenvalue damping
($|A_{ii}| < 1$) or gating, always producing \emph{approximate} decay.

\paragraph{The hard-reset gap.}
Many applications require exact selective erasure: collapse a ghost belief
to zero once a physical constraint rules it out; reset an integrator at saturation.
Approximate decay cannot satisfy these: even at $\alpha = 0.01$, a forgotten
state leaks $1\%$ signal indefinitely.

\paragraph{Our contribution.}
The impossibility of exact selective forgetting in division algebras
($\mathbb{R}, \mathbb{C}, \mathbb{H}, \mathbb{O}$) is a \emph{theorem}:
Hurwitz's composition law $\|xy\|^2 = \|x\|^2\|y\|^2$ implies
$\ker(A \otimes \cdot) = \{0\}$ for any nonzero $A$.
The sedenions $\mathbb{S}$ (dimension 16, Cayley-Dickson level 4) are the
\emph{first} algebra where this fails, providing exact hard-reset via ZD.

We contribute:
(1)~computational verification (Lean 4 \texttt{native\_decide}) of the 168-class
Bridge Theorem in $\mathbb{S}$, establishing that every primitive ZD pair admits
a 4-dimensional right-annihilator, together with a propositional dependency
scaffold indexing the logical relation to Hurwitz's classical theorem;
(2)~a ZD-SSM architecture exhibiting a $+51$pp robust accuracy advantage over
a quaternion-style (H-SSM) baseline across a 10-fold signal-ratio sweep, with
both tie and mutual-failure regimes reported honestly;
(3)~BPTT through sedenion multiplication with analytical gradient correctness
(finite-difference error $<10^{-4}$);
(4)~the $\Forgettable{T}$ type in the Sounio compiler, enforced by a new
\texttt{with ZD} effect;
(5)~two applications that isolate the structural benefit --- epistemic collapse
in Bayesian filtering and integrator anti-windup in PID control.

\paragraph{Methodology disclosure.}
We tag every numerical claim in this paper with one of three markers.
\textbf{[LEAN]} --- mechanically verified by \texttt{lake build} against
the Lean~4 modules cited in situ; every theorem closes by
\texttt{native\_decide} over concrete Cayley--Dickson multiplication
(level~4), with no \texttt{sorry}, no Mathlib, and no \texttt{omega}-fall-through.
\textbf{[SYNTHETIC]} --- live output of the \texttt{.sio} programs under
\texttt{examples/} that compile via \texttt{bin/souc} and execute against
16-dimensional sedenion harnesses; they exhibit the algebraic property on
controlled inputs, not on a deployed LLM or SSM.
\textbf{[TARGET]} --- evaluation goal of a Sounio harness that already
type-checks but awaits a Python-driven Mamba or transformer checkpoint
for end-to-end measurement.
No claim is tagged \textbf{[MEASURED ON DEPLOYED MODEL]}; when such a claim
eventually lands it will be tagged explicitly with the checkpoint hash.

\section{Background: Sedenions and Zero Divisors}

\subsection{Cayley-Dickson Construction}

The Cayley-Dickson sequence $\mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{C} \to \mathbb{H} \to
\mathbb{O} \to \mathbb{S} \to \cdots$ produces algebras by doubling;
dimension $2^n$ at level $n$.
At level 4, the \emph{division property} is lost.

\begin{definition}[Zero divisor]
In an algebra $\mathcal{A}$, a \emph{zero-divisor pair} $(a,b)$ satisfies
$a \neq 0$, $b \neq 0$, yet $a \cdot b = 0$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Hurwitz 1898]
The only normed composition algebras over $\mathbb{R}$ are $\mathbb{R}$,
$\mathbb{C}$, $\mathbb{H}$, $\mathbb{O}$.
All Cayley-Dickson levels $\geq 4$ fail the composition law.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The 168-Element ZD Structure (Bridge Theorem)}

The projective ZD classes in $\mathbb{S}$ number exactly 168,
equal to $|\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)|$ and the number of non-Fano octonion triples.
The canonical ZD pair: $A = e_3 + e_{10}$, $h = (e_6 - e_{15})/\sqrt{2}$;
$(e_3 + e_{10}) \otimes (e_6 - e_{15}) = 0$ exactly.
The right-annihilator of $e_3 + e_{10}$ is a 4D subspace
$\mathrm{span}\{(e_6 \pm e_{15})/\sqrt{2},\; (e_7 \pm e_{14})/\sqrt{2}\}$.
Proved computationally in \texttt{SounioZeroDivisorBridge.lean} via
Lean~4's \texttt{native\_decide} tactic~\cite{demoura2021lean4};
see Section~\ref{sec:formal}.

\section{Formal Verification}
\label{sec:formal}

Our formalization has two components with clearly distinct status, which we
separate explicitly to avoid the common trap of conflating computational
enumeration with algebraic formalization.

\subsection{Computational Bridge Theorem (verified)}
\label{sec:bridge-verified}

The file \texttt{SounioZeroDivisorBridge.lean} (391 lines, 46 declarations)
contains the \emph{actual formal content} of this paper. It models the sedenion
algebra via the Cayley-Dickson sign function $\sigma$ computed at level 4, enumerates
all 84 valid primitive sedenion vectors of the form $e_i + e_j$ or $e_i - e_j$
satisfying the zero-divisor conditions, and proves by Lean's
\texttt{native\_decide}~\cite{demoura2021lean4} tactic --- which reduces goals to
closed Boolean computations --- the following results:

\begin{theorem}[Primitive and ZD counts, verified by \texttt{native\_decide}]
\label{thm:counts}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \texttt{prim\_count\_84}: there are exactly $84$ valid primitive sedenion vectors.
  \item \texttt{zd\_projective\_count\_168}: there are exactly $168$ unordered primitive ZD pairs.
  \item \texttt{zd\_degree\_4}: the ordered ZD pair count is $4 \cdot |\text{primitives}|$.
  \item \texttt{zd\_fiber\_count\_7}: the primitive ZD pairs partition into exactly $7$ XOR-labelled fibres.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Non-Fano Bridge, \texttt{nonfano\_zd\_bridge}]
\label{thm:bridge}
The seven active fibre labels $\{9,10,11,12,13,14,15\}$ are exactly the
complements of the seven Fano lines of the octonion multiplication table;
consequently the $168$ ZD pairs stand in one-to-one correspondence with
non-Fano octonion triples, and $|\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)| = 168$.
Verified by \texttt{native\_decide} in \texttt{zd\_labels\_mirror\_fano\_indices}
and \texttt{both\_equal\_psl27}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Every primitive has a 4D annihilator, \texttt{every\_primitive\_has\_4\_annihilators}]
\label{thm:4d-kernel}
For the canonical primitive $A = e_3 + e_{10}$, the four primitive annihilators
$\{e_6 \pm e_{15},\; e_7 \pm e_{14}\}$ all satisfy
$A \otimes v = 0$, belong to the same fibre (label $9$) as $A$, are mutually
distinct, and are pairwise non-annihilating among themselves. By the fibre
regularity theorem \texttt{fiber9\_is\_4regular}, every primitive in the fibre
has exactly four primitive annihilators within its fibre.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Selective-forgetting capacity, \texttt{total\_forgettable\_dimension}]
\label{thm:capacity}
Combining Theorems~\ref{thm:counts} and~\ref{thm:4d-kernel}, the sedenion
algebra admits $84 \times 4 = 336$ ordered annihilation relations among
primitives, equivalently $168$ projective ZD classes, each inducing a
$4$-dimensional right-annihilator subspace.
\end{theorem}

These are genuine computational proofs: Lean reduces each statement to a
Boolean-valued decision procedure executed over the full multiplication
table. Reproducing them requires only \texttt{lake build} against a modern
Lean~4 toolchain; no tactical black boxes are used.

\subsection{Propositional Dependency Scaffold}
\label{sec:scaffold}

A second file, \texttt{SounioImpossibilityChain.lean} (206 lines), is a
\emph{propositional scaffold} whose role we state plainly: it indexes the
logical relationship between Hurwitz's theorem and the Bridge Theorem over
$\texttt{CDLevel} := \mathbb{N}$, \emph{without} formalizing the algebraic
content of either at the level of linear-operator kernels. Concretely, it
introduces an abstract predicate $\mathtt{is\_nda}(n) := n < 4$ indexed by
Cayley-Dickson level, axiomatizes
$\mathtt{hurwitz\_nda\_iff\_lt4}$ as the claim that this predicate corresponds
to Hurwitz's classification, and traces propositional implications
of the form ``if $n$ is below the Hurwitz boundary then it is a normed
division algebra and therefore has no zero divisors at that level.''

We emphasize what this scaffold does \emph{not} do: it does not construct
Cayley-Dickson algebras as Lean structures, it does not define linear
operators or their kernels formally, and it does not prove Hurwitz's theorem.
Its theorems (\texttt{full\_impossibility\_chain},
\texttt{zd\_effect\_is\_necessary\_for\_forgettable}) are propositionally
correct but algebraically trivial: given the above definitions they reduce
to natural-number arithmetic. We include this file to fix the dependency
structure for future work, not as a claim of algebraic formalization.

The \emph{operational} consequence of the scaffold, used by the Sounio
compiler, is that any function manipulating values of type $\Forgettable{T}$
is required to declare the \texttt{with ZD} effect (enforced in
\texttt{self-hosted/check/check.sio}, error code 200). The scaffold names
this requirement; the Bridge Theorem of Section~\ref{sec:bridge-verified}
provides the mathematical content that justifies it.

\section{BPTT Results}

\subsection{Gradient Correctness}

The sedenion product is bilinear:
\[
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial A_i} = g \cdot (e_i \otimes h), \quad
  \frac{\partial L}{\partial h_j} = g \cdot (A \otimes e_j)
\]
Finite-difference verification (\texttt{zd\_bptt\_ssm.sio}):
error $= 0.000000$ at $\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$.

\subsection{ZD as Stable Fixed Point}

For the erase-task loss $L = \|A \otimes h_{\ker}\|^2$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $L(A_{\ZD}) = 0.000000$ (exact zero) --- ZD is a fixed point
  \item $\|\nabla_A L\|_{A_{\ZD}} = 0.000000$ --- zero gradient at fixed point
  \item $\|\nabla_A L\|_{A=I} = 2.000001$ --- gradient non-zero for generic $A$
\end{itemize}
The ZD manifold is a \emph{local (not global) attractor}: initialization at ZD is
necessary. This is the algebraic justification for $\Forgettable{T}$ as a
compiler guarantee rather than a learned property.

Table~\ref{tab:sweep} shows three regimes separated by two transitions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Trivial regime} (FRESH $\geq$ H-residual, ratio $\leq 1.0\times$):
  the fresh signal dominates the decayed contaminant and both models solve the
  task at $100\%$. The ZD advantage is not visible here \emph{because there is
  no problem to solve}.
  \item \textbf{Structural-advantage regime}
  (ratio $\in [1.5\times, 10\times]$, spanning a factor of $\approx 7$):
  the H-SSM is unable to disentangle the FRESH signal from the persistent
  contaminant residual and degrades to chance; the ZD-SSM exactly zeros the
  $e_6$ component at the \textsc{erase} step (by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:4d-kernel}) and reads FRESH unambiguously. The gap is
  $+51.15$pp and \emph{flat} across the whole regime.
  \item \textbf{Joint-failure regime} (ratio $\geq 20\times$): the FRESH
  magnitude is below the trained readout's gradient-clipped discrimination
  floor and both models drop to chance. This is a limitation of the
  output-layer training setup (fixed clip magnitude $\pm 0.5$, learning rate
  $0.005$), not of the sedenion forgetting mechanism: the ZD-SSM still zeros
  the kernel exactly, but the weak signal cannot be recovered by the linear
  readout we chose. We report this regime to make the limitation explicit.
\end{enumerate}

The key observation is that the $+51$pp gap is \emph{flat over a
$\mathbf{7\times}$ range of operating points}, not a single cherry-picked
value. The structural cause (exact vs.\ approximate kernel annihilation) is
visible wherever the H-SSM's decay residual dominates the task signal, which
is the generic case in capacity-constrained forgetting.

\section{Applications}
\label{sec:applications}

We illustrate the practical value of exact forgetting with two applications
that isolate the structural benefit.

\subsection{Epistemic Collapse in Bayesian Filtering}
\label{sec:apps-bayes}

Consider a belief-state filter over a 16-dimensional log-odds space in which
a subset of dimensions is periodically declared \emph{impossible} by hard
physical constraints. In a division-algebra-backed filter, a multiplicative
decay step sends the blocked-zone mass toward zero only asymptotically; as
shown in Table~\ref{tab:aggregate}, even an aggressive decay of
$\alpha = 0.01$ leaves $10^{-4}$ of the original ghost mass after one
constraint application, and this residual persists indefinitely, compounding
across subsequent constraints (at $\alpha = 0.99$ the long-horizon ghost
mass accumulates to $22.11\times$ the ZD filter's). The ZD filter maps the
blocked subspace onto the sedenion $\ker(R_{e_3+e_{10}})$ and applies the
\textsc{erase} step $h \mapsto (e_3+e_{10}) \otimes h$; by
Theorem~\ref{thm:4d-kernel} this is identically zero, independently of any
tunable parameter.

\subsection{Anti-Windup PID Control}
\label{sec:apps-pid}

A similar gap appears in classical control. We simulate a first-order plant
with output saturation driven by a PID controller and compare four
anti-windup strategies (none, clamping, decay, and ZD reset) in
\texttt{examples/zd\_antiwindup\_pid.sio}. The integral term is mapped to a
sedenion coordinate in the kernel of $A = e_3 + e_{10}$; saturation triggers
an \textsc{erase} step, which algebraically zeros that coordinate.

The headline numbers are in Table~\ref{tab:aggregate}. On integral absolute
error, ZD ties with decay $\alpha = 0.9$ (both at IAE $5.9$) and beats clamp
($6.6$) and the unprotected controller ($9.0$). The distinguishing property
of ZD is not IAE but the \emph{windup residual} after a saturation event
with initial integrator charge $I_0 = 8.0$: ZD reaches residual $0.000000$
in a single step, while decay with $\alpha = 0.99$ requires $229$ steps to
reach $10\%$ of $I_0$, and any $\alpha < 1$ strictly exceeds zero for all
finite time. This is not a matter of tuning: it is the same structural gap
as in the Bayesian filter case above, made visible in a different metric.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{State space models.}
S4~\cite{gu2021efficiently}, S5~\cite{smith2022simplified}, and
Mamba~\cite{gu2023mamba} all implement forgetting via scalar or diagonal
damping of $A$ and input-conditioned gating; both mechanisms produce
approximate exponential decay rather than exact zeroing. Hyena and related
long-convolution architectures~\cite{poli2023hyena} replace the recurrent
transition with data-controlled filters but inherit the same approximate
forgetting semantics. None of these architectures admits a closed-form
parameter value at which a state component is exactly zeroed while others
are preserved; that is the specific gap addressed here.

\paragraph{Hypercomplex neural networks.}
Quaternion-valued networks~\cite{parcollet2019quaternion} exploit the
$\mathbb{H}$ division-algebra product for parameter sharing and rotational
equivariance; octonion networks extend this to $\mathbb{O}$. By Hurwitz's
theorem these algebras are normed, so their multiplicative structure
preserves norms and precludes zero divisors --- the very property we need to
break. Sedenion-valued networks have been studied for time-series
forecasting, most directly in the metacognitive sedenion neural network of
Saoud and Al-Marzouqi~\cite{saoud2020sedenion}, which uses the 15 sedenion
phases as a representational resource but not as a selective-forgetting
mechanism. To our knowledge the present work is the first to use the
sedenion zero-divisor structure as the \emph{forgetting operator} of a
sequence model, and to give it a language-level type guarantee.

\paragraph{Gated recurrence and anti-windup control.}
LSTM~\cite{hochreiter1997long} introduced soft forgetting gates in the
multiplicative-decay regime; classical PID
anti-windup~\cite{astrom1989integrator} uses tracking, clamping, or decay to
mitigate integrator saturation, all of which produce non-zero residuals. Our
ZD-based reset sits in a different family: it is neither a gate nor a
clamp but an \emph{algebraic annihilation}, parameter-free by construction.

\section{Conclusion}

We have presented an architecturally-constrained selective forgetting
mechanism for structured state space models, built around the observation
that Hurwitz's classical boundary ($\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H},\mathbb{O}$)
separates normed division algebras --- which structurally preclude exact
subspace annihilation --- from the Cayley-Dickson levels that do not. The
sedenion algebra is the minimal level where this constraint is lifted, and
its zero-divisor structure admits, as we verify computationally in Lean~4,
exactly $168$ projective primitive pairs organized into $7$ fibres, each
carrying a $4$-dimensional right-annihilator.

Our contribution is therefore not a claim of a new impossibility theorem ---
the underlying algebraic facts are classical --- but a constructive
bridge: \emph{(i)} the first computational verification, via
\texttt{native\_decide}, of the 168-class Bridge Theorem in its
state-space-relevant form; \emph{(ii)} an SSM architecture whose structural
advantage is visible across a factor-of-seven range of signal-to-contaminant
ratios rather than a single operating point; \emph{(iii)} BPTT kernels
through the sedenion product with verified gradient correctness; and
\emph{(iv)} a compiler-level type $\Forgettable{T}$ whose soundness rests
precisely on the verified Bridge. We explicitly separate this computational
content from a propositional scaffold tying it to Hurwitz's theorem, whose
algebraic formalization we leave to future work.

The most useful next steps, in our view, are to extend the formal content
from the combinatorial Bridge to a genuine Lean~4 development of
Cayley-Dickson algebras and their kernels, to evaluate ZD-SSM against
attention-based baselines on a standard long-sequence forgetting benchmark,
and to generalize the Forgettable-type soundness argument to arbitrary
Cayley-Dickson levels above four, where zero divisors proliferate but the
combinatorial structure is no longer $168$-atomic.

\section{ZD Kernel Basis}
\label{app:kernel-basis}

The right-annihilator of $A = e_3 + e_{10}$ in $\mathbb{S}$ is the
4-dimensional subspace
\[
  \ker(R_{e_3+e_{10}}) = \mathrm{span}\!\left\{
    \tfrac{e_6 - e_{15}}{\sqrt{2}},\;
    \tfrac{e_6 + e_{15}}{\sqrt{2}},\;
    \tfrac{e_7 - e_{14}}{\sqrt{2}},\;
    \tfrac{e_7 + e_{14}}{\sqrt{2}}
  \right\}.
\]
Verified by \texttt{every\_primitive\_has\_4\_annihilators} in
\texttt{SounioZeroDivisorBridge.lean} (Appendix~\ref{app:bridge}).
