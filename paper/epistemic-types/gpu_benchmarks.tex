\subsection{GPU Performance: Epistemic GEMM}
\label{sec:gpu-perf}

To evaluate epistemic types on GPU hardware, we implement an epistemic
GEMM kernel that propagates \know{f32} values through matrix
multiplication.  Each element carries four \emph{shadow registers}: the
value, its GUM standard uncertainty~$\eps$, a validity predicate (killed
by near-zero division), and a provenance tag.  Arithmetic in the inner
loop follows the GUM's law of propagation of uncertainty:
addition/subtraction uses quadrature ($\eps_c = \sqrt{\eps_a^2 +
\eps_b^2}$), multiplication uses first-order propagation ($\eps_c =
|a|\eps_b + |b|\eps_a$).

The \sounio compiler generates PTX targeting SM~7.0 (forward-compatible
with Ada Lovelace), dispatched via the CUDA Driver API on an NVIDIA~L4
(Ada Lovelace, 30.3~TFLOPS peak FP32, 23~GB VRAM).
Table~\ref{tab:gpu-perf} reports results over 19~passing runs across
four matrix dimensions.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\caption{Epistemic GEMM performance on NVIDIA L4 (SM~7.0 PTX, Series~A).
  GFLOPS = $2MNK / (t \times 10^{-3}) / 10^9$; \emph{Peak~\%} is
  fraction of the L4's 30.3~TFLOPS FP32 peak.}
\label{tab:gpu-perf}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
Dimension & $n$ & Median GFLOPS & Range & Peak~\% \\
\midrule
$1024^3$ & 1 & 6{,}061 & ---         & 20.0 \\
$2048^3$ & 3 & 7{,}201 & 6{,}786--7{,}585 & 25.0 \\
$4096^3$ & 6 & 5{,}253 & 5{,}155--5{,}606 & 17.3 \\
$8192^3$ & 3 & 4{,}876 & 4{,}355--4{,}957 & 16.1 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The epistemic GEMM achieves a median of 5.3~TFLOPS at $4096^3$,
corresponding to 17.3\% of the L4's peak FP32 throughput---notable given
that every multiply-accumulate is accompanied by uncertainty propagation
arithmetic.  The 2048 sweet spot (25\% peak) likely reflects better
occupancy at moderate tile sizes.  Performance scales from 1024 to 8192
without degradation below the 500~GFLOPS threshold, and 8192-scale
stability is tight (mean 4{,}730~GFLOPS, $\sigma = 267$, CV~5.6\%).
PTX target selection affects throughput by $\sim$20\%: SM~7.0 generic
PTX (15{,}647~chars) outperforms on-host-generated PTX
(22{,}580~chars) due to reduced register pressure.

\paragraph{Limitation.}
We do not yet have a plain SGEMM baseline on the same hardware,
so we cannot quantify the overhead of the four shadow registers per
element.  A cuBLAS comparison and shadow-register ablation study are
planned for the camera-ready version.
