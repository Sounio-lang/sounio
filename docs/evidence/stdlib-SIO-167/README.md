# Genuine noncentral t, directional power and checked minimum sample size

This package replaces the implicit shifted-central-t power approximation with genuine noncentral-t rejection probabilities. It depends on the checked Normal/Beta façade and canonical stable Beta work in PR3102, scientific parent e1b176a20914f1ecbc268635d005103346e2d198. The reviewed PR3102 current tip 99264f6617d78d03edfef7fb365dd75956eb8b1e was then merged at a84c27fa572e to keep stacked review ancestry explicit. Its own branch merged origin/main c21b0e139830ca4949f80cba62887a8922a972d7 at 0a7f9fb3f8cc07974d83333351dd649a0d88389d. Dependency inclusion is explicit until human review/merge; this package does not merge main.

## Public contract

- stats::noncentral_t exposes central_t_tails_checked, noncentral_t_tails_checked and noncentral_t_quantile_checked. CDF, survival and both logarithms have separate availability flags. Underflow of a positive probability is unavailable in the scalar field; its finite log remains available. Infinite support endpoints are exact mathematical endpoints. Quantiles report a bracket and the observed forward log tail at the actual returned binary64 value.
- stats::checked_power exposes power_onesample_checked, power_twosample_checked, n_for_power_onesample_checked and n_for_power_twosample_checked. One-sided means a signed right-tail alternative. Two-sided uses both rejection regions and is symmetric in the effect sign. Two-sample n is per group, with df=2n−2 and noncentrality d sqrt(n/2); one-sample uses df=n−1 and d sqrt(n).
- stats::power preserves the four original scalar names/signatures. Supported outputs are corrected; invalid, unavailable, exhausted or unverified results assert rather than returning a plausible fallback. The retained approximation is accessible only through the explicitly named power_ttest_onesample_shifted_t_approx and power_ttest_twosample_shifted_t_approx. These two functions retain the older central-Student kernel and its documented approximations; checked power never calls them.

Status 0 means the selected bounded algorithm completed its stopping checks. It is not a theorem, a universal error certificate or a guarantee that every finite valid input is supported. Status 1 refuses invalid domains/options; 2 refuses nonfinite parameters/NaN; 3 reports work or depth exhaustion; 4 reports arithmetic failure; 5 refuses unsupported work regions, unavailable intermediates or an unresolved numerical decision. Sample-size status 6 reports an actual evaluated cap that did not attain the target, while status 7 reports a symbolic unreachable target (zero effect above alpha, or a wrong-direction one-sided alternative at/above alpha). Failure does not expose a fabricated available minimum. A power refusal after evaluation preserves all performed critical/tail work; a separately computed critical value can remain available even when the requested power is unavailable.

The allowed outer parameters are finite df in (0,2000000], absolute noncentrality at most 128, budget 1..1000000 and tolerance 8 epsilon..1e−4. The opposite-direction integral additionally requires df at least 0.5 and absolute t at most 1000000. Central Cauchy uses its analytic atan/cot expressions, including a nonzero subnormal tail at t=1e308. Other combinations can refuse when Beta coordinates or noncentrality squared become unrepresentable. No normal or shifted-t fallback is hidden in an unsupported region. Numerical qualification below samples this declared envelope; it does not validate every combination in it.

## Algorithms and budgets

For a nonnegative oriented effect, the positive Poisson/incomplete-Beta mixture begins at floor(delta²/2) and visits both directions. Integer and half-integer mode weights use the additive canonical gamma_log_prefix_checked helper to avoid cancellation in the log weight. Every visited pair consumes an outer mixture term; canonical Beta iterations are separately counted. A geometric omitted-weight estimate controls stopping relative to the requested direct probability. Stable logarithmic addition is used, and a direct probability above 0.9 is explicitly refused instead of taking an unstable complement.

For an opposite-direction effect, the survival probability is the positive conditional integral E[NormalSF(t sqrt(V/df)−delta)], with V chi-square(df). Sounio integrates in r=log(V/2), uses a bounded peak search, logarithmic scaling, a geometric panel mesh and adaptive Gauss7/Kronrod15 quadrature with depth 20. Lower/upper truncation estimates combine canonical Gamma tails with monotone Normal tail factors. Both the final aggregate quadrature error estimate and the truncation estimate must be small relative to the computed tiny tail. Per-leaf absolute goals avoid resolving negligible tails indefinitely; the final aggregate check remains mandatory. These are numerical estimates, not proved rigorous bounds.

The integral's quadrature_evaluations counter includes the initial Normal factor, mode-search and integrand calls. truncation_evaluations counts actual individual Gamma/Normal truncation calls, including partial failed checks. Their sum is capped by the supplied budget. kernel_iterations records actual underlying special-function iterations. Mixture outer terms are capped by budget, with up to two budget-limited Beta kernels per term plus the initial Normal kernel. Thus budget is hierarchical, not a single global count of elementary operations. General noncentral quantile evaluations count actual CDF calls; central quantile evaluations count Beta-inverse steps plus the final forward check. Each nested kernel is independently bounded by budget. The public sample-size evaluations counter counts actual checked-power calls. Memory is bounded by scalar state and integral recursion depth; no sample-size, tail-grid or Poisson-weight arrays are allocated.

The additive Gamma prefix reuses centered deviance/log1pmx and Stirling corrections for shape at least 8, with the existing small-shape log-Gamma path otherwise. Existing Gamma P/Q consumers are unchanged. It is qualified separately on 78 actual native prefix/mode rows, including fractional large modes and the shape-8/path boundaries. Its observed absolute log-prefix error at huge magnitudes differs from its much smaller mode-weight error; neither is represented as an all-shape certificate.

Sample-size search evaluates actual powers on an exponential bracket and integer binary search. An available n greater than 2 requires actual cached power(n−1) < target <= power(n), with the computed decision margin larger than 16 tolerance. That guard is conservative, not a certified bound on power error. A target too close to numerical equality is unavailable. At n=2, n=1 is outside the model; the result explicitly marks the lower domain boundary and does not invent power(1). Wrong-direction targets below alpha are resolved only when the actual n=2 already meets them; other negative-direction minimum searches refuse instead of assuming increasing power.

## Independent oracle qualification

The ordinary mixture grid uses Boost with 113-bit precision and an extended exponent range. Power, critical values, general NCT quantiles and minimum-neighbor checks use an independent 512-bit Boost calculation. Raw signed i64 bit patterns from native Sounio are decoded as binary64; the checker reads actual executable output, not expected values copied into the fixture. The three C++23 reference sources generate typed Sounio fixtures and verify their output.

Installed Boost's signed noncentral-t series was itself an invalid oracle for some rare opposite-direction tails: one retained row yielded a negative probability, while another exceeded the exact NormalSF(−delta) upper bound for t>0 and delta<0. [Rejected oracle diagnostics](positive_control/rejected_signed_oracle.stderr) preserve those observations; they are not accepted expected values. A separate positive integral in binary128 uses a chi-to-z² coordinate, Boost Gauss/Kronrod61, two refinement tolerances and an independent Gamma/Normal truncation check. The coordinate and quadrature rule differ from Sounio's. Both independent refinements must agree to relative 1e−18 and their estimated error must meet the same level; truncation must be at most 1e−20 relative. An oracle that fails these controls throws instead of weakening the comparison tolerance. No arbitrary-precision normal approximation replaces the genuine integral.

## Method sources

[Boost's noncentral-t documentation](https://www.boost.org/doc/libs/1_88_0/libs/math/doc/html/math_toolkit/dist_ref/dists/nc_t_dist.html) documents the Normal/chi-square definition and the bidirectional Poisson/Beta approach associated with Benton and Krishnamoorthy. The [current primary implementation discussion](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/boostorg/math/develop/include/boost/math/distributions/non_central_t.hpp) also describes a positive conditional-integral route for cancellation-prone tails. The Sounio integral and its budgets were implemented independently; no Boost C++ source was translated or copied. Standard Gauss/Kronrod nodes and weights and the distribution identities are mathematical data. The canonical prefix follows the [DLMF log-Gamma asymptotic expansion](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11.E1). Boost is linked only in the independent C++ verification programs, under its existing license.

Raw toolchain and executable stderr/stdout captures remain byte-preserved, including g++ version output with a trailing blank line. A whitespace-only diff warning in those raw receipts is not rewritten to conceal provenance. Executable reference source and maintained documentation are checked separately.
