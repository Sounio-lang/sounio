# The 168 Theorem: PSL(2,7) Governs Non-Associativity and Zero-Divisor Structure in the Cayley–Dickson Tower

## Abstract

We prove that the number of non-associative basis triples in the octonion algebra is exactly 168 = |PSL(2,7)|, the order of the automorphism group of the Fano plane. The result follows from the regular (sharply transitive) action of PSL(2,7) on ordered non-collinear triples of the Fano plane, which are precisely the triples whose associator is nonzero. We establish a *binary property*: for imaginary unit basis elements $e_i, e_j, e_k$, the associator norm $\|[e_i, e_j, e_k]\|$ takes only the values $\{0, 2\}$ — no intermediate magnitudes exist. This dichotomy extends to the sedenions (dimension 16), where all nonzero associator counts among basis elements are multiples of 168: the full sedenion count is $1848 = 11 \times 168$, with the doubling grade $i \oplus j \oplus k = 8$ alone carrying exactly 168. The number of sedenion zero-divisor pairs $(e_i \pm e_j)(e_k \pm e_l) = 0$ is exactly $336 = 2 \times 168$, exactly matching the sed-sed nonzero associator count (the (lower, upper, lower) cross-pattern count is independently also 336). We then develop two new structural theories. First, the *canonical zero-divisor graph* $G_b$ of the level-$b$ Cayley–Dickson algebra is exactly solvable: $G_4$ is the crown graph $K_{7,7}$ minus a perfect matching plus one isolated vertex, and we give closed forms for the degree sequence, independence number $\alpha(G_b) = b + 4$, and clique and chromatic numbers $\omega(G_b) = \chi(G_b) = 2^{b-3}$ — proved for all $b$ from the zero-divisor fiber structure, and verified exhaustively against exact scans through level 8 and out of sample at level 9 (512 dimensions). Second, we state the *nullity histogram law* (verified at levels 4–8 with two consecutive out-of-sample confirmations at levels 8 and 9; the all-$b$ induction remains open): the kernel-dimension spectrum of every canonical zero divisor is generated by a fiber-type invariant $\tau$ with label count law $A(m,\tau) = 7 + v_2(\tau)$ and an aggregate generation law $\mathrm{mult}_b(k) = 6 \cdot 2^{b-k} f(k)$, $f(k) = (2k-5)2^{k-3} + 1$, which strictly refines the census law $Z(b) = 4^b - (3b-1)2^b + 2^{b-1} - 4$. All enumerations are verified exhaustively in the Sounio programming language and by independent exact Python contracts; the path product norm invariance theorem, the sedenion zero-divisor census (84 primitives, 336 ordered pairs, 168 projective classes), and the sedenion associator census (1848 = 11 × 168) are machine-checked in Lean 4 with no `sorry`.

**Keywords:** octonions, sedenions, associator, Fano plane, PSL(2,7), Cayley–Dickson, zero divisors, zero-divisor graph, nullity spectrum, Lean 4

**MSC 2020:** 17A75 (Composition algebras), 20B25 (Finite automorphism groups of algebraic, geometric, or combinatorial structures), 17D05 (Alternative rings), 05C25 (Graphs and abstract algebra)

## 1. Introduction

The Cayley–Dickson construction produces a tower of algebras by iterated doubling:

$$\mathbb{R} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{C} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{H} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{O} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \mathbb{S} \xrightarrow{\times 2} \cdots$$

At each step, a structural property is lost: commutativity at the quaternions $\mathbb{H}$, associativity at the octonions $\mathbb{O}$, and the division property at the sedenions $\mathbb{S}$ (which acquire zero divisors) [1, 2]. The octonions are the last normed division algebra (Hurwitz, 1898) and have found deep applications in string theory [3], exceptional Lie groups [4], and quantum information [5].

The multiplication table of the octonions is encoded by the Fano plane — the unique projective plane of order 2, with 7 points and 7 lines (each containing 3 points). The automorphism group of the Fano plane is PSL(2,7) $\cong$ GL(3, $\mathbb{F}_2$), a simple group of order 168 [6]. This group acts on the octonion basis elements by permuting them while preserving the multiplication structure.

While the role of PSL(2,7) in octonion multiplication has been extensively studied [1, 4, 7], a remarkably simple combinatorial fact appears to have escaped explicit statement in the literature:

> **The number of ordered triples $(i,j,k) \in \{1,\ldots,7\}^3$ for which the octonion basis associator $[e_i, e_j, e_k] = (e_i e_j)e_k - e_i(e_j e_k)$ is nonzero is exactly 168.**

This is not a coincidence — it is a consequence of PSL(2,7) acting *regularly* (sharply transitively) on ordered non-collinear triples of the Fano plane. We develop this observation and trace it through the Cayley–Dickson tower, finding that the number 168 serves as the fundamental quantum of non-associativity at the octonion and sedenion levels, and that the zero-divisor geometry it seeds admits an exact graph-theoretic and spectral description at *every* level of the tower.

### Contributions

1. **The 168 Theorem** (Section 2): The nonzero basis associator count equals |PSL(2,7)|, with a complete decomposition: 343 = 133 (alternativity zeros) + 42 (Fano-line zeros) + 168 (nonzero).

2. **The Binary Property** (Section 3): $\|[e_i, e_j, e_k]\| \in \{0, 2\}$ exactly, for both octonions and sedenions. No intermediate values exist.

3. **The Tower Extension** (Section 4): All sedenion nonzero associator counts are multiples of 168. Sub-decomposition: oct-oct = 168, sed-sed = 336 = 2 × 168, cross = 1344 = 8 × 168, total = 1848 = 11 × 168. The doubling grade $i \oplus j \oplus k = 8$ carries exactly 168; the 455 unordered support triples split into 35 associative, 168 semi-associative (2 of 6 orderings), and 252 fully non-associative (all 6 orderings) — all machine-checked in Lean 4.

4. **The Zero-Divisor Coincidence** (Section 5): The number of sedenion zero-divisor pairs $(e_i \pm e_j)(e_k \pm e_l) = 0$ is 336 = 2 × 168, exactly matching the cross-half nonzero associator count. The full counting chain 42 edges → 84 primitive elements → 336 ordered pairs → 168 projective classes is machine-checked in Lean 4, together with the fiber structure: 7 xor-fibers with labels $\{9, \ldots, 15\}$, 12 vertices and 48 internal pairs each, in bijection with the 7 Fano lines.

5. **The Zero-Divisor Graph Invariant Theory** (Section 6): The canonical zero-divisor graph $G_b$ is exactly solvable at every level $b \geq 4$. We prove a crown-join recursion (recovering $G_4 = K_{7,7}$ minus a perfect matching, plus the isolated generator $e_8$), a closed-form degree law, generator isolation, $\alpha(G_b) = b + 4$, and $\omega(G_b) = \chi(G_b) = 2^{b-3}$ via an explicit Thue–Morse clique and an 8-block coloring. Verified exhaustively against an audited exact scan at levels 4–8 and out of sample at level 9.

6. **The Nullity Histogram Law** (Section 7): The nullity (kernel-dimension) spectrum of canonical zero divisors is generated exactly by a fiber-type invariant $\tau(\ell) = (r - \mathrm{lowbit}(r)) \gg 3$ with label count law $A(m,\tau) = 7 + v_2(\tau)$ ($\tau \geq 1$), a fiber histogram recursion, and the aggregate generation law $\mathrm{mult}_b(k) = 6 \cdot 2^{b-k} f(k)$. This explains the previously unexplained multiplicities $\{684, 504, 336, 870\}$ of the level-7 histogram and strictly refines the census law.

## 2. The 168 Theorem

### 2.1 Setup

Let $\{e_0, e_1, \ldots, e_7\}$ denote the standard octonion basis, where $e_0 = 1$ is the identity and $e_1, \ldots, e_7$ are imaginary units. The multiplication is determined by the Fano plane: for each oriented line $(i,j,k)$, we have $e_i e_j = e_k$ and $e_j e_i = -e_k$.

The **associator** of three octonions is defined as:
$$[a, b, c] = (ab)c - a(bc)$$

The octonions are *alternative*: $[a, a, b] = [a, b, b] = 0$ for all $a, b$. By the Artin theorem, any subalgebra generated by two elements is associative [1].

### 2.2 Counting nonzero basis associators

Consider all $7^3 = 343$ ordered triples $(i,j,k) \in \{1,\ldots,7\}^3$. We classify them:

**Layer 1: Repeated indices (133 zeros).** If any two of $\{i,j,k\}$ coincide, the associator vanishes by alternativity ($[a,a,b] = 0$) or flexibility ($[a,b,a] = 0$, which follows from alternativity in alternative algebras [1]). By inclusion-exclusion over the three pairwise-equality conditions:

$$|\{i=j\}| + |\{j=k\}| + |\{i=k\}| - |\{i=j=k\}| \cdot \binom{3}{2} + |\{i=j=k\}| = 3(49) - 3(7) + 7 = 133$$

**Layer 2: Fano-line triples (42 zeros).** Among the $7 \times 6 \times 5 = 210$ triples with all-distinct indices, those where $\{i,j,k\}$ forms a line of the Fano plane generate a quaternion subalgebra, which is associative. There are 7 lines, each contributing $3! = 6$ ordered triples:

$$7 \times 6 = 42$$

**Layer 3: Non-collinear triples (168 nonzero).** The remaining triples have all-distinct indices and do not lie on any Fano line:

$$343 - 133 - 42 = 168$$

### 2.3 The group-theoretic explanation

**Theorem 1 (H, L).** *The number of nonzero basis associator triples equals* $|\mathrm{PSL}(2,7)|$.

*Proof.* The Fano plane $\mathbb{F}$ has 7 points and 7 lines. An **ordered non-collinear triple** is an ordered triple $(p,q,r)$ of distinct points not all on a line. The count of such triples is $7 \times 6 \times 5 - 7 \times 6 = 168$ (total ordered distinct triples minus those on a common line).

PSL(2,7) $\cong$ Aut($\mathbb{F}$) acts on ordered non-collinear triples. We claim this action is **regular** (free and transitive):
- *Transitive*: PSL(2,7) acts transitively on ordered bases of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ (ordered triples of vectors spanning the space), and non-collinear triples in $\mathbb{F}$ correspond to ordered bases of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ under the identification $\mathbb{F} \cong \mathbb{P}(\mathbb{F}_2^3)$.
- *Free*: The stabilizer of an ordered basis of $\mathbb{F}_2^3$ in GL(3, $\mathbb{F}_2$) is trivial (an invertible linear map is determined by its action on a basis).

Since PSL(2,7) $\cong$ GL(3, $\mathbb{F}_2$), the action is regular, and the number of orbits is 1. Therefore:

$$|\{\text{nonzero associator triples}\}| = |\{\text{ordered non-collinear triples}\}| = |\text{PSL}(2,7)| = 168. \qquad \square$$

The count is independently confirmed in Lean 4 (`oct_168` in `SounioSedenionAssociator1848.lean`, by `native_decide` over the explicit Cayley–Dickson sign table) and agrees with the Sounio enumeration `examples/octonion_168_associators.sio`.

## 3. The Binary Property

**Theorem 2 (H, E).** *For all $i, j, k \in \{1, \ldots, 7\}$:*
$$\|[e_i, e_j, e_k]\| \in \{0, 2\}$$

*Proof.* For basis elements $e_i, e_j$ with $i \neq j$ and both $\geq 1$, the product $e_i e_j = \pm e_m$ for some $m \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$ (by the Fano plane multiplication rule). Therefore:

$$(e_i e_j) e_k = (\pm e_m) e_k = \pm e_p \quad \text{for some } p$$
$$e_i (e_j e_k) = e_i (\pm e_n) = \pm e_q \quad \text{for some } q$$

Each term is $\pm$ a basis element. The associator $[e_i, e_j, e_k] = \pm e_p \mp e_q$, which is either:

- **Zero**, if $p = q$ and the signs match, or
- **$\pm 2 e_r$**, if $p = q$ and the signs oppose (giving $\|\pm 2e_r\| = 2$).

It remains to show that $p \neq q$ never occurs (i.e., the two parenthesizations always land on the *same* basis element). In the Cayley–Dickson sign-table model, both parenthesizations land on index $i \oplus j \oplus k$ (the sign table satisfies $e_a e_b = \sigma(a,b)\, e_{a \oplus b}$), so $p = q = i \oplus j \oplus k$ always. $\square$

Exhaustive confirmation: all 343 octonion and all 3375 sedenion basis associator norms are computed exactly in Sounio (`examples/oct_conjecture_test.sio`, Conjecture 3; `examples/sedenion_168_verify.sio`); the only values attained are 0 and 2. The Lean formalization of the underlying sign structure (`SounioCayleyDickson.lean`) makes the $\oplus$-index law definitional.

**Remark.** The binary property means that non-associativity for basis elements is a **parity phenomenon** — a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ flip, not a continuous rotation. The associator does not move the result to a different dimension of the algebra; it reflects it within the same dimension. Section 8 makes the companion statement precise: although the *direction* of a product depends on parenthesization, its *norm* does not (path product norm invariance, machine-checked in Lean 4).

## 4. The Tower Extension

We extend the analysis to the sedenions $\mathbb{S}$ (dimension 16), constructed by Cayley–Dickson doubling of $\mathbb{O}$: $\mathbb{S} = \mathbb{O} \oplus \mathbb{O}\ell$, where $\ell = e_8$ and $e_9, \ldots, e_{15}$ are the "sedenion extension" basis elements.

### 4.1 Sub-decomposition

Among all $15^3 = 3375$ ordered triples of imaginary sedenion basis elements:

| Sub-class | Range | Total triples | Nonzero | Factor of 168 |
|-----------|-------|---------------|---------|---------------|
| Oct-oct-oct | $i,j,k \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$ | 343 | 168 | $1 \times 168$ |
| Sed-sed-sed | $i,j,k \in \{8,\ldots,15\}$ | 512 | 336 | $2 \times 168$ |
| Cross (mixed) | at least one from each half | 2520 | 1344 | $8 \times 168$ |
| **Total** | $i,j,k \in \{1,\ldots,15\}$ | **3375** | **1848** | **$11 \times 168$** |

**Theorem 3 (E, L).** *Every entry in the "Nonzero" column is a multiple of 168.*

The binary property (Theorem 2) extends to sedenions: exhaustive computation over all 3375 triples confirms $\|[e_i, e_j, e_k]\| \in \{0, 2\}$ for all sedenion basis elements. The total 1848 is machine-checked in Lean 4 (`total_1848`, by `native_decide`), as is the octonion subcount (`oct_168`). Note that sedenions are not alternative; the enumeration confirms that repeated-index basis triples nonetheless contribute zero (every pair of basis elements generates an associative quaternionic subalgebra), so restricting to distinct indices loses nothing.

### 4.2 The grade refinement

The xor **grade** $g = i \oplus j \oplus k$ stratifies the 1848. Machine-checked in Lean 4:

- **Grade 8** (the doubling grade) carries exactly **168** ordered non-associative triples (`grade8_168`) — the same count as the entire octonion level.
- The remaining 14 grades carry **1680 = 10 × 168** together (`other_1680`), so $1848 = (10 + 1) \times 168$.

At the unordered level, the $\binom{15}{3} = 455$ support triples decompose by how many of their 6 orderings are non-associative:

| Class | Orderings nonzero | Count | Mass |
|-------|-------------------|-------|------|
| Associative | 0 of 6 | 35 | 0 |
| Semi-associative | 2 of 6 | 168 | 336 |
| Fully non-associative | 6 of 6 | 252 | 1512 |
| **Total** | | **455** | **1848** |

Furthermore, *every* grade-8 support triple is fully non-associative (`grade8_all_full`), which is why grade 8 carries $168 = 28 \times 6$ while the other grades share $120 = 16 \times 6 + 12 \times 2$ each. Precisely: grade 0 carries no non-associative triple at all ($i \oplus j \oplus k = 0$ is the collinearity condition), grade 8 carries 168, and each of the 14 remaining grades $\{1,\ldots,7,9,\ldots,15\}$ carries exactly 120. The semi-associative class itself has cardinality 168 — a fourth, independent appearance of the constant inside the sedenion associator census.

### 4.3 The sed-sed count

The sed-sed count of 336 = 2 × 168 admits a structural explanation. The sedenion "upper half" $\{e_8, \ldots, e_{15}\}$ carries an induced multiplication structure from the Cayley–Dickson doubling. The product $e_{i+8} \cdot e_{j+8}$ involves two octonion multiplications (from the Cayley–Dickson formula $(a,b)(c,d) = (ac - \bar{d}b, da + b\bar{c})$), introducing a factor of 2 in the non-associativity count relative to the octonionic base.

## 5. The Zero-Divisor Coincidence

The sedenions are the first Cayley–Dickson algebra containing zero divisors: nonzero elements $z, w$ with $zw = 0$ [2, 8].

### 5.1 Enumeration

We exhaustively search all pairs of the form $z = e_i + s_1 e_j$, $w = e_k + s_2 e_l$ with $i < j$, $k < l$, $s_1, s_2 \in \{+1, -1\}$, and $i, j, k, l \in \{0, \ldots, 15\}$.

**Result (E, L):** Exactly **336** such pairs satisfy $zw = 0$.

$$336 = 2 \times 168 = 2 \times |\text{PSL}(2,7)|$$

### 5.2 The counting chain

The 336 decomposes into a rigid chain, every link machine-checked in Lean 4 (`SounioZeroDivisorBridge.lean`, all by `native_decide`):

$$42 \text{ edges} \;\longrightarrow\; 84 \text{ primitive elements} \;\longrightarrow\; 336 \text{ ordered pairs} \;\longrightarrow\; 168 \text{ projective classes}$$

- A **primitive** canonical zero-divisor element is a unit two-support imaginary element $v = e_i + s \cdot e_j$ with $i \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$ (lower half), $j \in \{9,\ldots,15\}$ (upper half, $e_8$ excluded), $s \in \{\pm 1\}$. There are exactly **84** (`prim_count_84`).
- The **336** ordered zero-divisor pairs $(u,v)$, $u \neq v$, $uv = 0$, satisfy $336 = 4 \times 84$: every primitive element annihilates exactly 4 others (`zd_pair_count_336`, `zd_degree_4`).
- Identifying opposite orderings leaves exactly **168** unordered (projective) classes (`zd_projective_count_168`).

### 5.3 The fiber structure and the Fano mirror

Grouping primitive elements by xor-label $\ell = i \oplus j$:

- Exactly **7 labels** occur: $\ell \in \{9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15\}$ (`zd_fiber_count_7`, `zd_fiber_labels`).
- Each fiber contains exactly **12** primitive vertices (`zd_fiber_sizes`) and exactly **48** internal ordered zero-divisor pairs (`zd_fiber_internal_pairs`; $7 \times 48 = 336$, and every zero-divisor pair is intra-fiber, `zd_pairs_intra_fiber`).
- The 7 fiber labels mirror the 7 Fano lines: the Fano lines satisfy $i \oplus j \oplus k = 0$ (`fano_line_xor_zero`), the fiber labels are their $8$-shifts, and the counts match (`zd_labels_mirror_fano_indices`, `seven_fibers_seven_lines`).

The **bridge theorem** (`nonfano_zd_bridge`, `both_equal_psl27`) records the equality of the two 168s inside Lean 4: the 168 non-Fano (non-associative) octonion triples and the 168 projective zero-divisor classes of the sedenions.

### 5.4 The coincidence

The sedenion zero-divisor count (336) exactly equals the sed-sed nonzero associator count (336). Both are $2 \times |\text{PSL}(2,7)|$. Independently, the cross-pattern triples $(i, j, k)$ with $i \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$, $j \in \{8,\ldots,15\}$, $k \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$ — $392 = 7 \times 8 \times 7$ ordered triples — also contain exactly 336 nonzero associators (`examples/sedenion_168_verify.sio`, re-verified against the Lean sign table in the course of this work), so the constant 336 appears on both sides of the tower break and inside the cross pattern.

This establishes a precise numerical link between the two fundamental breakdowns in the Cayley–Dickson tower:

- **Non-associativity** (loss of $(ab)c = a(bc)$): governed by ordered non-collinear triples of the Fano plane.
- **Zero divisors** (existence of $ab = 0$ with $a, b \neq 0$): governed by pairs of elements in orthogonal sub-octonions.

Both are controlled by the same group-theoretic structure — PSL(2,7), the automorphism group of the Fano plane — appearing at scale 1× for octonionic non-associativity and at scale 2× for sedenion zero divisors and upper-half non-associativity.

**Remark.** de Marrais [9] independently counted 168 "primitive unit zero-divisors" in the sedenions, arranged in 42 "Assessors." Our 336 counts *ordered pairs*, so $336 = 2 \times 168$ is consistent with his enumeration (each de Marrais zero-divisor generates two ordered pairs with opposite sign choices). The fact that this number equals the upper-half nonzero associator count appears to be new, as does the Lean-checked fiber structure of §5.3.

## 6. The Zero-Divisor Graph Invariant Theory

The coincidence of Section 5 is the level-4 shadow of a tower-wide structure. Define the **canonical zero-divisor graph** $G_b$ of the level-$b$ Cayley–Dickson algebra (dimension $2^b$): vertices are the imaginary units $e_1, \ldots, e_{2^b - 1}$, and $\{i,j\}$ is an edge iff $a = e_i \pm e_j$ is a zero divisor (by sign duality both signs always occur together). The full development, with all proofs and the exact-scan verification harness, is the companion technical report [12]; we state the main results with their proof ideas.

Recall that a **ZD label** is an integer $\ell \geq 8$, not a power of two, written $\ell = 2^{m-1} + r$, $1 \leq r \leq 2^{m-1} - 1$, with **birth level** $m = \lfloor \log_2 \ell \rfloor + 1$. The fiber structure (birth fiber plus embedded/high doubling) was established in [13] and is verified exhaustively at levels $b \leq 8$ and out of sample at level 9.

**Theorem 4 (H; E at $b = 4..9$).** *Crown-join recursion.* With $h = 2^{b-1}$, $L = [1, h)$, $H = [h, 2^b)$:

1. $G_b$ restricted to $L$ is $G_{b-1}$; restricted to $H$ it is $G_{b-1}$ shifted by $h$;
2. the cross pair $\{x, h + y\}$ ($x \in [1,h)$, $y \in [0,h)$) is a zero-divisor pair iff $y \neq 0$ and $x \neq y$ — the cross edges form the **crown graph** $K_{h-1,h-1}$ minus the perfect matching $\{\{x, h+x\}\}$ (the matched pairs have label $h$, a power of two, hence invertible);
3. $e_h$ is isolated.

At $b = 4$ this says $G_4$ is exactly the crown graph $K_{7,7}$ minus a perfect matching, plus the isolated vertex $e_8$: the 7 lower and 7 upper units pair off completely except along the diagonal $\{i, i+8\}$. In particular $G_4$ has $42 = 168/4$ edges — the combinatorial root of the counting chain of §5.2.

**Theorem 5 (H; E at $b = 4..9$).** *Degree law.* For every $b \geq 4$ and $i \in [1, 2^b)$, with $s = v_2(i)$ the 2-adic valuation:

$$\deg_b(i) = \sum_{m = \max(4,\, s+2)}^{b} \left(2^{m-1} - 2\right) = (2^b - 2b - 2) - \max\!\big(0,\; 2^{s+1} - 2s - 4\big).$$

*Proof idea.* Vertex $i$ has exactly one partner ($i \oplus \ell$) for each $m$-born label with $i \bmod 2^{m-1} \notin \{0, r\}$, and none otherwise; the constraint excludes exactly one of the $2^{m-1} - 1$ values of $r$ per birth level, and the geometric series closes as stated. $\square$

**Corollary (generator isolation).** $\deg_b(2^{b-1}) = 0$: the last Cayley–Dickson generator $e_{2^{b-1}}$ is the *unique* isolated vertex of $G_b$ — it participates in no canonical zero divisor at all.

**Corollary (census identity).** $\sum_i \deg_b(i) = Z(b)$, reproducing the census law

$$Z(b) = 4^b - (3b-1)\,2^b + 2^{b-1} - 4$$

as an exact integer identity (checked through $b = 64$; at $b = 9$ it gives $Z(9) = 249084$, matching the exact scan [14]).

**Theorem 6 (H; E at $b = 4..12$).** *Independence law.* $\alpha(G_b) = b + 4$ for all $b \geq 3$, realized by

$$A_b = \{1, \ldots, 7\} \cup \{2^t : 3 \leq t \leq b-1\}$$

(the octonion units plus the tower generators).

*Proof idea.* In the complement $\bar{G}_b$ (the "invertible graph"), the cross pairs are exactly the star $\{\{x, h\}\}$ and the perfect matching $\{\{x, h+x\}\}$ — both have label $h$, a non-ZD label. The generator $h$ is universal in $\bar{G}_b$, and a clique case analysis gives $\omega(\bar{G}_b) = \omega(\bar{G}_{b-1}) + 1$ with $\omega(\bar{G}_3) = 7$ (the octonions are a division algebra), so $\alpha(G_b) = \omega(\bar{G}_b) = 7 + (b-3) = b+4$. The set $A_b$ attains the bound: its cross pairs are exactly the defect-diagonal pairs missing from every fiber. $\square$

**Theorem 7 (H; E at $b = 4..12$).** *Clique and chromatic law.* $\omega(G_b) = \chi(G_b) = 2^{b-3}$ for all $b \geq 4$.

*Proof idea.* Upper bound: every ZD label satisfies $\ell \geq 8$, so every edge crosses distinct aligned 8-blocks, and $i \mapsto i \gg 3$ is a proper $2^{b-3}$-coloring. Lower bound: the **Thue–Morse clique**

$$C_b = \{\, c(k) = 8k + 6 + \mathrm{TM}(k) : 0 \leq k < 2^{b-3} \,\}, \qquad \mathrm{TM}(k) = \mathrm{popcount}(k) \bmod 2,$$

is a clique: for $k \neq k'$ the pair label $c(k) \oplus c(k')$ satisfies the zero-divisor criterion of [12, Theorem B] because $c(k) \bmod 8 \in \{6,7\}$ can never collide with the excluded residues $\{0, r\}$ (which are $\equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 8$). Hence $2^{b-3} \leq \omega(G_b) \leq \chi(G_b) \leq 2^{b-3}$. $\square$

**Verification status (E).** Every statement above is verified exhaustively against the audited exact 2-cycle scan at levels 4–8 (all pairs, ZD and non-ZD) and **out of sample at level 9** (512 dimensions, 124542 index pairs, $Z(9) = 249084$ reproduced, 0 mismatches). The independence and clique numbers are independently confirmed by exact branch-and-bound at $b = 4..8$; the contract harness is `scripts/research/cd_zd_graph_invariants_contract.py` (clauses T1–T8, verdict `C_GREEN`).

To our knowledge this invariant theory is new: prior literature treats the sedenion level only (Moreno's classification [2]; Cawagas [8]; de Marrais's box-kites [9]), and zero-divisor graphs of commutative rings [15] are a disjoint tradition.

## 7. The Nullity Histogram Law

Each canonical zero divisor $a = e_i \pm e_j$ has a left-multiplication operator $L_a$ whose kernel dimension (nullity) measures how much of the algebra it annihilates. The level-7 census [13] tabulated the exact nullity histogram

$$\{4{:}684,\; 8{:}504,\; 12{:}504,\; 16{:}336,\; 20{:}336,\; 24{:}336,\; 28{:}336,\; 32{:}336,\; 36{:}336,\; 40{:}336,\; 44{:}336,\; 48{:}504,\; 52{:}504,\; 56{:}684,\; 60{:}870\}$$

and explicitly left the multiplicities unexplained. The following law explains them. Full details and proofs-as-verified are in the companion report [16].

**Setup.** The nullity of $L_a$ for an $m$-born pair at level $b$ is $2^{b-m+2} \cdot t$ with $t$ odd, $1 \leq t \leq 2^{m-3} - 1$ [13]. Reparametrize the odd part by $u = (t-1)/2 \in [0, 2^{m-4} - 1]$, so the nullity at birth level is $8u + 4$. Write $\mathrm{lowbit}(r) = r \mathbin{\&} (-r) = 2^{v_2(r)}$.

**Theorem 8 (E at $b = 4..8$; out of sample at $b = 9$).** *Fiber type invariant.* The nullity histogram of the fiber of $\ell = 2^{m-1} + r$ at every level $b \geq m$ depends only on $(m, \tau(\ell), b)$, where

$$\tau(\ell) = (r - \mathrm{lowbit}(r)) \gg 3 \in [0, 2^{m-4} - 1].$$

Lifting is exact: the level-$b$ histogram equals the birth-level histogram with nullities *and* multiplicities scaled by $2^{b-m}$. The number of labels born at level $m$ of fiber type $\tau$ is

$$A(m, \tau) = \begin{cases} m + 3 & \tau = 0 \\ 7 + v_2(\tau) & 1 \leq \tau \leq 2^{m-4} - 1 \end{cases}$$

— independent of $m$ for $\tau \geq 1$. (Consistency: $\sum_\tau A(m,\tau) = 2^{m-1} - 1$, the number of labels born at $m$, via the classical identity $\sum_{\tau=1}^{2^p - 1} v_2(\tau) = 2^p - p - 1$.)

**Theorem 9 (E at $b = 4..8$; out of sample at $b = 9$).** *Aggregate generation law.* At level $b \geq 4$, the multiset of nullities of canonical zero-divisor index pairs consists of exactly $2^{b-k}$ distinct nullity values, each occurring with multiplicity

$$\mathrm{mult}_b(k) = 6 \cdot 2^{b-k} \cdot f(k), \qquad f(k) = (2k-5)\,2^{k-3} + 1,$$

for each **generation** $k = 4..b$. Since a nullity's 2-adic valuation determines its birth class, generations never collide.

The level-7 multiplicities are thereby explained: $336 = 6 \cdot 2^3 \cdot f(4) = 48 \cdot 7$ (8 values), $504 = 6 \cdot 2^2 \cdot f(5) = 24 \cdot 21$ (4 values), $684 = 6 \cdot 2 \cdot f(6) = 12 \cdot 57$ (2 values), $870 = 6 \cdot f(7) = 6 \cdot 145$ (1 value, the maximum nullity 60). The generation sequence $f(k) = 7, 21, 57, 145, 353, 833, 1921, \ldots$ is a new numerical invariant of the tower.

**Corollary (census refinement).** The histogram law sums to the census law: $Z(b) = 12 \sum_{k=4}^{b} 4^{b-k} f(k)$, since $Z(b) - 4 Z(b-1) = 12 f(b)$ and $Z(4) = 84 = 12 f(4)$. The multiplicity law is a strict refinement of the census — it *contains* the census as its total mass (not an independent derivation: both sides descend from the same fiber recursion).

**Out-of-sample falsification tests survived.** The law's level-8 predictions ($Z(8) = 59772$, 243 fibers, 31 distinct nullities, maximum 124, multiplicity multiset $\{672{:}16, 1008{:}8, 1368{:}4, 1740{:}2, 2118{:}1\}$) were made before the level-8 scan ran and confirmed exactly. The level-9 predictions ($Z(9) = 249084$; multiplicity multiset $\{1344{:}32, 2016{:}16, 2736{:}8, 3480{:}4, 4236{:}2, 4998{:}1\}$; 63 distinct nullities) were likewise made in advance and confirmed exactly by an independent exact scan with a full GF(65521) rank audit of all 260610 pair-sign matrices, 0 mismatches [14, 17].

**Honesty note.** Theorems 8–9 are exact laws verified exhaustively (exact integer arithmetic, no floating point) over all 441 fibers at levels 4–8, with two consecutive out-of-sample confirmations. A complete induction for all $b$ is sketched in [16] but not yet complete: what remains open is a closed cocycle computation controlling how the sign pattern of the *new* native labels at level $m$ reflects that of level $m-1$, and a coordinate-free meaning of the fiber type $\tau$ (why the lowest set bit of $r$ and the three bits above it drop out). We state the law for the verified levels and as the natural conjectural extension beyond them.

### 8.1 Path product norm invariance

The **path product norm invariance theorem** (`path_product_norm_invariant`): for any two parenthesizations (full binary trees) $t_1, t_2$ of a path product through an octonion-labeled graph with the same leaves in the same order,

$$\| \mathrm{eval}(t_1) \|^2 = \| \mathrm{eval}(t_2) \|^2 .$$

The proof is structural induction on the tree, applying norm multiplicativity at each internal node (`evalTree_normSq_eq_prodLeaves`: the norm-squared of any parenthesization equals the product of the leaf norms). Corollaries cover the 3-element associator pair and all five Catalan parenthesizations of 4 elements. The companion witness theorems (`associator_nonzero_witness`, `associator_witness_norm_equal`) exhibit the exact sense in which octonion non-associativity is directional but norm-preserving: $(e_1 e_2) e_4 \neq e_1 (e_2 e_4)$ as elements, with equal norms. This is the graph-theoretic content behind the binary property of Section 3.

## 10. Discussion

### 10.1 PSL(2,7) as a universal constant

The number 168 now appears at five distinct places in the Cayley–Dickson tower:

1. As the count of nonzero octonion basis associators (Theorem 1).
2. As the count of sedenion projective zero-divisor classes, in bijection-by-count with the non-Fano triples (bridge theorem, §5.3).
3. As the count of semi-associative unordered sedenion support triples, and as the associator mass of the doubling grade 8 (Section 4).
4. As the denominator in Wilmot's formula $T_n = (2^n - 1)(2^n - 2)(2^n - 4)/168$ for Cayley–Dickson automorphism counting [11].
5. As one-half the sedenion zero-divisor pair count and the sed-sed nonzero associator count.

This suggests that PSL(2,7) is not merely the automorphism group of the Fano plane — it is the **fundamental structural constant** governing algebraic breakdown in the Cayley–Dickson tower. The Fano plane encodes how seven imaginary units can multiply consistently; PSL(2,7) counts the ways they can *fail* to associate consistently. Sections 6–7 show what grows past the Fano plane: above level 4 the structure is no longer group-theoretic but *graph- and spectral-theoretic*, governed by the xor-birth structure of the doubling, with closed forms ($\alpha = b+4$, $\omega = \chi = 2^{b-3}$, $\mathrm{mult}_b(k) = 6 \cdot 2^{b-k} f(k)$) that hold at every computed level.

### 10.2 Physical interpretation

The Cayley–Dickson tower corresponds to physical regimes of decreasing algebraic regularity:

| Level | Algebra | Physics | Breakdown |
|-------|---------|---------|-----------|
| $\mathbb{R}$ | Reals | Classical mechanics | — |
| $\mathbb{C}$ | Complex | Quantum amplitudes | Commutativity |
| $\mathbb{H}$ | Quaternions | SU(2) gauge (weak force) | Ordering |
| $\mathbb{O}$ | Octonions | String theory transverse dimensions | Associativity |
| $\mathbb{S}$ | Sedenions | Beyond the division algebra boundary | Zero divisors |

The binary property (Theorem 2) implies that non-associativity at the basis level is a **discrete, $\mathbb{Z}_2$ phenomenon**, not a continuous deformation. In the octonion-to-sedenion transition, both non-associativity and zero divisors emerge with counts controlled by PSL(2,7), suggesting a unified algebraic mechanism underlying both breakdowns. This table is interpretive, not a physical claim.

### 10.3 Scope and limitations

- All zero-divisor results concern **canonical** two-unit sums $e_i \pm e_j$, not the full zero-divisor variety (which is infinite beyond the canonical locus [2]).
- The graph invariant theorems of Section 6 are proved from the fiber structure of [13]; that fiber structure is itself established by induction from the block recursion and verified exhaustively at levels $b \leq 8$ with an out-of-sample confirmation at level 9. The nullity histogram law of Section 7 is verified at the same levels; its all-$b$ induction remains open (§7, honesty note).
- The Cayley–Dickson sign convention is the repository's standard doubling convention; other sign conventions give isomorphic multiplication tables only in low levels.
- The sedenion census counts are fully machine-checked in Lean 4; the higher-level scans (levels 5–9) are exact integer computations audited by an independent GF(65521) rank verification, not yet formalized in a proof assistant.

### 10.4 Open questions

1. **The associator side above level 4.** The zero-divisor side of the tower is now exactly solved at every level (Sections 6–7). The *associator* side is not: is there a closed form for the number of non-associative basis triples at level $b \geq 5$ (dimension 32 and beyond)? The 1848 = 11 × 168 decomposition and its grade structure (Section 4) are the level-4 anchor of this question.

2. **From coincidence to bijection.** Is there an explicit *bijection* (not just a numerical coincidence) between sedenion zero-divisor pairs and nonzero sed-sed associator triples? The fiber structure of §5.3 (7 fibers of 48 pairs, mirroring the 7 Fano lines) is the natural scaffold for such a map.

3. **The factor 11.** The grade refinement decomposes 1848 = 168 + 1680 with the doubling grade carrying exactly 168. Is the residual factor 10 = 1680/168 — which does not divide |PSL(2,7)| — a shadow of a higher-level symmetry group, or an artefact of the xor-grading? The semi-associative class having cardinality exactly 168 hints at further hidden structure.

4. **The fiber type.** What is the intrinsic, coordinate-free meaning of $\tau(r) = (r - \mathrm{lowbit}(r)) \gg 3$ — why do the lowest set bit and the three bits above it drop out of the nullity histogram?
