## Abstract

We study the failure of composition in the Cayley–Dickson tower as a computable geometric
object, and we build a disciplined bridge from that object to the stratification theory of
catastrophe-theoretic semantics. On the algebraic side: the zero-divisor set of a
Cayley–Dickson algebra is the singular locus {det L_x = 0} of left multiplication; it is
empty for ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆 and is born at the doubling 𝕆 → 𝕊, where the canonical two-unit
zero divisors number exactly 84 (Cawagas' count, reproduced) and organise into 7 fibres of
12, indexed by the Fano plane. We verify Koebisu's exact factorisation det L_x = D₁⁴D₂² to
10⁻¹⁴, and give a proof, verified by executable contract, that the determinant has quartic
contact with the codimension-4 zero-divisor locus. We extend the census to the
trigintaduonions (level 5, 588 zero divisors in 22 fibres) and the 64-dimensional chingons
(level 6, 3036 zero divisors in 53 fibres), and exhibit closed-form fibre-count and
fibre-size laws reproducing all three levels exactly — stated as an inductive law with an
explicit level-7 falsifier, not as a theorem. On the bridge side: near any Fano line the
octonion associator jet is linear and single-axis; a first-principles map Φ_fp sends the
jet's even part to the depth and its odd part to the tilt of the cusp potential's control
plane, realising Petitot's two opposition moves (contrariety, contradiction) as distinct
path classes. Lifting this to a functor F from associator data to stratification invariants,
we prove its uniformity over the seven lines, locate and repair a G₂-covariance obstruction
in the polar coordinate, show the cross-line correction of F is exactly the G₂ 3-form φ,
and prove the resulting invariant tower {δ, φ, ψ} closes under contraction at order 2 (no
further independent algebraic 3- or 4-form invariant is generated by the octonion core). Two further
cross-column probes are honest negatives with located mechanisms: the butterfly stratum is
obstructed (we prove φ vanishes on all coupling jets), and Ollivier–Ricci curvature is
blind to the algebra's oriented content on the canonical incidence graphs. A positive
cross-order result closes the arc: one Fano plane and the full PSL(2,7) (all 168
collineations, transitive) index the φ-lines, the zero-divisor fibres, and the secondary
ternary operation simultaneously. Finally, we place φ inside the exceptional tower —
concretely, as the imaginary restriction of the E₆/Albert cubic cross-term — and state
precisely why the Wildgen semantic conjecture about E₆–E₈ remains a conjecture. Every
load-bearing claim is discharged by a named executable contract (twenty-two contracts, all
re-run for this submission); no identity between the algebra and any semantic potential is
asserted.

## 1. Introduction

Two research traditions mark the failure of composition with a *negative* object. In
catastrophe-theoretic semantics (Thom, Petitot), rupture is the crossing of a bifurcation
set: a family of potentials degenerates where a Hessian determinant vanishes. In the
structuralist tradition more broadly, obstruction appears as a missing map, an unglued
section, a diverging metric. The starting observation of this paper is that the
Cayley–Dickson tower supplies *positive, graded, computable* objects for the same
phenomenon: the octonion **associator** (a nonvanishing alternating 3-form-related tensor,
order-1 rupture) and the sedenion **zero-divisor determinant** det L_x (a nonnegative
graded quantity that reaches zero exactly at annihilation, order-2 rupture).

The paper has four goals.

**Goal 1 — the catastrophe, computed.** We instrument the birth and growth of the
zero-divisor locus across the Cayley–Dickson doubling tower. The locus is empty on the
division algebras and is born at 𝕆 → 𝕊; the canonical two-unit zero divisors are counted
exactly at levels 4, 5, 6 (84, 588, 3036) and their fibre structure is determined
completely at those levels, including a closed-form growth law with a stated falsifier.

**Goal 2 — a theorem, not just a measurement.** Using Koebisu's factorisation of det L_x
(attributed, §3.2), we prove that the determinant has quartic contact with the
zero-divisor locus, upgrading an earlier Monte-Carlo measurement to a proof whose every
analytic step is checked by an executable contract.

**Goal 3 — the bridge, with claim discipline.** We construct Φ_fp and its functorial lift
F from octonion associator jets to the stratification of the cusp potential, and we
establish what kind of statement the bridge supports. The programme's own claim ladder
(§2.4) forbids the unqualified identity "the semantic bifurcation set *is* the algebraic
locus" (called D3); what we prove is a G₂-equivariant, jet-functorial correspondence,
together with *located* accounts of where the correspondence provably stops.

**Goal 4 — the exceptional frontier, honestly bounded.** We show the G₂ 3-form φ at the
centre of the bridge is the imaginary restriction of the E₆/Albert cubic invariant's
octonion cross-term, and that the "clean cross-term" phenomenon tops out below E₈. The
semantic conjecture this inspires (Wildgen's four-actant morphology and the exceptional
singularities) is stated as a conjecture, with its landmine named — not as a result.

The methodological position is that obstructions are first-class results: four of our
contract rungs are *characterisations of failure*, each locating its mechanism. A
Falsification Ledger (§8) makes the programme's controlled negatives citable artifacts.

## 2. Mathematical background

### 2.1 The Cayley–Dickson tower

The Cayley–Dickson construction iterates the doubling A → A ⊕ A with product
(a,b)(c,d) = (ac − d̄b, da + bc̄), producing ℝ, ℂ, ℍ, 𝕆, 𝕊 (sedenions, dim 16),
𝕋 (trigintaduonions, dim 32), 𝕀 (chingons, dim 64; level 6), and so on. Each doubling
loses a structural property: ordering, then commutativity (at ℍ), associativity (at 𝕆),
and the division property (at 𝕊). We use the canonical basis e_0 = 1, e_1, …, e_{n−1} with
the XOR-indexed sign law: e_i e_j = σ(i,j) e_{i⊕j}, σ(i,j) ∈ {±1}, computed by the
standard recursive Cayley–Dickson rule. All computations in this paper use this basis; every
contract re-audits the multiplication core against the doubling axioms before use.

### 2.2 Left multiplication and the zero-divisor locus

For x in a Cayley–Dickson algebra A, write L_x : A → A, y ↦ x·y. Then, immediately from
the definitions:

**Proposition 2.1 (D0).** x is a zero divisor if and only if L_x is singular, if and only
if det L_x = 0. ∎

The zero-divisor set is therefore the singular locus of a family of linear operators
parametrised by x — formally the same kind of object as Thom's bifurcation set of a family
of potentials parametrised by controls (this is a structural parallel of families, not a
variety isomorphism; see §2.4). For the division algebras, det L_x = |x|^dim never vanishes
off the origin. The locus is born at the 𝕆 → 𝕊 doubling.

For the sedenions, write x = (x_0 + u, x_8 + w) with u, w ∈ Im 𝕆 ≅ ℝ⁷, and set
A = |u|², B = |w|², γ = ⟨u,w⟩, C = x_0² + x_8².

**Theorem 2.2 (Koebisu).** With D₁ = |x|² = C + A + B and D₂ = D₁² − 4(AB − γ²),

  det L_x = D₁⁴ D₂² = (D₁² D₂)² ≥ 0.

We verified this factorisation numerically against direct 16×16 determinants: maximum
relative error 1.4 × 10⁻¹⁴ over random unit sedenions (contract clause T1). The
factorisation and the cone parametrisation are Koebisu's; our contribution is the
verification and the contact-order consequence (Theorem 3.3). Expanding,

  D₂ = C² + 2C(A+B) + (A−B)² + 4γ²,

a sum of four independent nonnegative terms; hence {det L_x = 0} is the complete
intersection {x_0 = x_8 = 0, A = B, γ = 0}, of codimension 4. In particular

  ZD₁(𝕊) ≅ V₂(ℝ⁷) ≅ G₂/SU(2),  dim 11,

the Moreno parametrisation of the unit zero divisors as 2-frames in Im 𝕆; the nonzero
locus is its cone, codimension 4 in ℝ¹⁶.

### 2.3 G₂, the Fano plane, and the forms φ and ψ

Aut(𝕆) = G₂, dim 14. The imaginary octonions carry the G₂-invariant 3-form
φ(x,y,z) = ⟨xy, z⟩ and its Hodge dual 4-form ψ = ∗φ. The associator
[x,y,z] = (xy)z − x(yz) is the vector-valued companion: on basis units,
[e_a, e_b, e_c] = −2ψ_{abcd} e_d; the associator vanishes exactly on associative triples.

The Fano plane PG(2,2) — 7 points, 7 lines, each line a triple {i, j, i⊕j} — indexes the
octonion structure constants: the 7 lines are the 7 associative triples of imaginary basis
units, i.e. the 7 quaternion subalgebras of 𝕆. The full collineation group is
PSL(2,7) = GL(3,2), of order 168. Two distinct lines meet in exactly one point. Of the
(7 choose 3) = 35 triples of imaginary units, 7 are lines (associator 0) and 28 are
non-lines (‖[e_i,e_j,e_k]‖ = 2). The 4-form ψ is nonzero (and calibrates to ±1) on exactly
7 of the 35 four-subsets: the line complements, the coassociative 4-planes.

For standard Cayley–Dickson algebras of dimension ≥ 16, the automorphism group remains G₂
(Eakin–Sathaye), so G₂ — and its Fano-collineation subgroup PSL(2,7) — acts at every level
of the tower we scan.

### 2.4 The claim ladder: what "the same event" may mean

The sentence "semantic and epistemic rupture are the same singularity" is ambiguous, and
the programme disciplines it into levels:

| Level | Statement | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **D0** | x is a zero divisor ⇔ L_x singular ⇔ det L_x = 0 | Proposition 2.1 |
| **D1** | {L_x} degenerating on {det = 0} is a bifurcation set in Thom's *operational* sense (a family of operators degenerating on a subvariety) | structural parallel — language of families, not an isomorphism |
| **D2** | the catastrophe set is empty on ℍ, 𝕆 and born at 𝕆 → 𝕊 (84/210 canonical two-unit sums), growing under further doubling | computed (§3) |
| **D3** | a Petitot semantic potential's bifurcation set *is* (or is dual to) the zero-divisor locus or the associator | **not established; asserted nowhere in this paper** |

The referee-safe content of this paper is D0–D2 plus the functorial correspondence F of
§§4–5 and the located obstructions of §6. D3 remains forbidden throughout.

### 2.5 The cusp potential

Petitot realises semantic oppositions as the critical-point structure of potentials. The
cusp V(x; a, b) = x⁴/4 + a x²/2 + b x has fold (bifurcation set) Δ = 4a³ + 27b² = 0 in the
control plane (a, b): over the bistable region two wells (the contraries) coexist; crossing
the fold merges or selects them. Two topologically distinct moves matter: *contrariety*
(both contraries can dissolve into a neutral term) and *contradiction* (one pole deepens
as the other vanishes; "neither" is impossible). A Boolean lattice 2² has a single
complement type and cannot host both moves — Petitot's non-Booleanisability obstruction,
carried by the topology of the strata.

## 3. The Cayley–Dickson catastrophe

### 3.1 Birth of the locus (D2)

Scanning all canonical two-unit sums e_i ± e_j (i < j) at each level:

| Algebra | dim | candidates | canonical zero divisors | density |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ℍ | 4 | 6 | 0 | 0 |
| 𝕆 | 8 | 42 | 0 | 0 |
| 𝕊 | 16 | 210 | **84** | 0.400 |
| 𝕋 | 32 | 930 | **588** | 0.632 |
| 𝕀 | 64 | 3906 | **3036** | 0.777 |

The count 84 at level 4 reproduces Cawagas (2004) exactly and cross-validates the XOR
basis against the standard sedenion zero-divisor structure; annihilation was verified
directly (‖a·b‖ ≈ 10⁻¹⁶ for structured partners, against a vanishing rate for random
mixed pairs). The catastrophe set — in Thom's operational, family-degeneracy sense (D1) —
is empty for the division algebras and appears at the 𝕆→𝕊 doubling; it then grows
monotonically in density.

### 3.2 The sedenion fibres

The 84 projective zero-divisor primitives of 𝕊 decompose into **7 fibres of 12** (42
positive-sign representatives, 168 edges, 42 quartets), indexed by the xor-label
ℓ = i ⊕ j ∈ {9,…,15}; each fibre is bipartite 6+6, every vertex of degree 4, and **all
annihilating pairs are intra-fibre** (INTRA_BAD = 0 over the census). Exact rational
measure on the canonical slice gives Var(product coordinates) = 0 on the locus and 1/150
off it (Frente A exact-arithmetic lineage). Contracts: `rupture_r2_fiber_measure_contract.py`
(R2 partial PASS), `sedenion_zd_fibers` gates.

### 3.3 Theorem A: quartic contact with the locus

Let d(x) = dist_{S¹⁵}(x, ZD₁(𝕊)) and d_sing(x) = |det L_x|^{1/16}/‖x‖ (scale-free;
vanishes exactly on the locus).

**Theorem 3.3 (quartic contact).** On S¹⁵, det L_x ≍ d(x)⁴ near ZD₁(𝕊); equivalently
d_sing(x) ~ c·t^{1/4} along transversal arcs x(t) = p + t·u.

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.2, det L_x = D₁⁴D₂² with D₁ = 1 on the unit sphere, so
det L_x = D₂². On S¹⁵ the zero set of D₂ = C² + 2C(A+B) + (A−B)² + 4γ² is the complete
intersection {C = 0, A − B = 0, γ = 0}. The gradients of the four defining functions are
independent on the locus (verified: minimum singular value of the gradient matrix = 1.0,
clause T4), so D₂ is a positive-definite quadratic form in the transversal directions:
D₂ ≍ d(x)². Hence det L_x ≍ d(x)⁴. The fitted slope of log det against log d along
transversal arcs is 3.994 (clause T6), and the vanishing order interpolated in exact
arithmetic on all 168 primitive edges is exactly 4 with leading coefficient 256 (clause
A2+ of the full tubular probe). ∎

**Remark 3.4.** The primitives are *non-generic* high-contact points of the discriminant
hypersurface: at every primitive, det L_x = 0 with rank L_x = 12 (corank 4, clause A1) —
not the simple corank-1 crossing of a generic fold. This upgrades the earlier
`R2_FULL_MEASURED` status (exact anchors plus Monte-Carlo) to a proof bounded by
executable verification (`r2_continuous_law_theorem_contract.py`, T_GREEN, 6/6 clauses).

### 3.4 Theorem B: the tower census and the fibre laws

At level b ≥ 4, call ℓ = i ⊕ j the label of a canonical pair; pairs sharing a label form a
fibre. Computed exactly at levels 4, 5, 6 (contracts T1–T6, C1–C7):

**(i) Fibre-count law.** A label ℓ supports zero divisors at level b iff ℓ ≥ 8 and ℓ is
not a power of two; hence

  F(b) = 2^b − b − 5  →  F(4) = 7, F(5) = 22, F(6) = 53.

**(ii) Birth-level size law.** Every label has a birth level m = ⌊log₂ ℓ⌋ + 1, and

  size(m, b) = 2^b − 2^(b−m+2),

verified fibre by fibre at b = 4, 5, 6 (level-6 stratification: 7 𝕊-born fibres of size
48; 15 𝕋-born of size 56; 31 𝕀-born of size 60). Schafer doubling doubles each annihilator
per level, so older fibres grow; native fibres are born at size 2^b − 4.

**(iii) Census law.** Summing (ii) over (i),

  Z(b) = Σ_{m=4}^{b} (2^{m−1} − 1)(2^b − 2^(b−m+2))
       = 4^b − (3b − 1)·2^b + 2^{b−1} − 4,

reproducing 84, 588, 3036 exactly.

**(iv) The defect diagonal.** Each fibre is almost full: of the 2^(b−1) − 1 candidate index
pairs per label (×2 signs), exactly 2^(b−m+1) − 1 are missing, and the missing pairs are
explicit. Writing ℓ = 2^(m−1) + r (1 ≤ r ≤ 2^(m−1) − 1),

  missing(ℓ, b) = { {a, a ⊕ ℓ} : a ∈ span_𝔽₂{r, 2^m, 2^{m+1}, …, 2^{b−1}} ∖ {0} }.

The fundamental missing pair is {r, 2^(m−1)}: the generator adjoined at the birth level
never annihilates its lower-half partners, and doubling propagates the defect upward
(1 missing pair at birth, 3 after one lift, 7 after two). Every present pair carries both
signs (sign duality).

**(v) Tower embedding.** The 𝕊 census equals the 𝕋 census restricted to indices < 16, and
the 𝕋 census equals the 𝕀 census restricted to indices < 32; labels are nested.

**Status statement (no overclaim).** Items (i)–(v) are *computed facts* at levels 4, 5, 6,
derived from a doubling heuristic, not yet proved inductively. The law predicts
Z(7) = 13 884 at level 7 (the 128-dimensional algebra); a level-7 scan is the stated
falsification target, and the falsifiers document specifies the demotion protocol ("exact
at b ≤ 6") should it fail. A plain quadratic interpolant in 2^b also fits three points but
predicts 13 692 — it is a different function, and only the inductive law (iii), as the sum
of the fibre-by-fibre laws (i)–(ii), is claimed.

**Remark 3.5.** The clean 7-fibre Fano indexing of §3.2 is sedenion-specific: the fibre
count grows 7 → 22 → 53. What persists up the tower is the *symmetry*: the diagonal lift
(g, g, g, g) of a Fano collineation is an automorphism of 𝕋 (verified, residual 0.0) and
permutes the level-5 zero-divisor set, consistently with the in-house orbit theorem that
the 168-element group acts on the growing fibres at all levels.

## 4. The Fano jet and the map Φ_fp

### 4.1 The jet lemma

**Lemma 4.1 (jet lemma).** Fix a Fano line L⋆ = {e_i, e_j, e_k} with e_i e_j = e_k. For
any off-line unit e_u (u ∉ {i,j,k}) and ε ∈ ℝ,

  [e_i + ε e_u, e_j, e_k] = ε · [e_u, e_j, e_k],

and the pure-triple associator [e_u, e_j, e_k] = ±2 e_m is single-axis: on the worked line
(1,2,3) with (i, j, k) = (1, 2, 3), the triples [e_u, e_2, e_3] for u = 4,5,6,7 give
±2e_5, ∓2e_4, ∓2e_7, ±2e_6 respectively. (Sign table in the recursive Cayley–Dickson
convention of §2.1; the 16 signed octonion multiplication tables differ by sign flips and
permutations, so numerically different tables in other conventions are the same statement
in different coordinates — the harness re-audits the core before use.)

*Proof.* The associator is ℝ-trilinear and [e_i, e_j, e_k] = 0 on a Fano line, so only the
ε-term survives; the single-axis, magnitude-2 values are the Cayley–Dickson sign law
computed (and re-audited) on the octonion core. ∎

So ambient coupling to one off-line direction produces an associator that is pure,
directed, and linear in ε: α(ε) = ±2ε e_m, with ‖α‖ = 2|ε| and signed component
α_m = ±2ε. The *direction* is the choice of u; the norm alone erases the sign.

### 4.2 Φ_fp: first-principles map to the cusp control plane

**Definition 4.2 (Φ_fp).** With the cusp potential V(x; a, b), set

  a = A₀ + ‖α‖²/4,   b = τ + α_m/2,   A₀ = −1.

Here A₀ (opposition depth) and τ (semantic tilt) are *semantic* inputs — the isolated
square's own data — and the even jet ‖α‖²/4 = ε² and odd jet α_m/2 = ±ε are the only
𝕆-derived deformation of the control plane. A₀ = −1 is a choice of unit placing the pure
square at the textbook symmetric bistable point, not a fit; on the pure table both jets
are measured in the same dimensionless ε-units as the neighbourhood coordinate, and no
coefficient is fitted to force a crossing.

**Theorem 4.3 (C; two path classes from one jet — R3_GREEN).** Under Φ_fp:

- **Path C (contrariety).** Dial τ = −α_m/2, so b ≡ 0. Sweeping ε: the system starts
  bistable with symmetric poles at ±1, both wells approach the origin, and ends
  monostable *neutral* (x = 0). Both contraries can dissolve — "both false" occurs.
- **Path D (contradiction).** Dial τ = 0, so b = α_m/2. Sweeping ε: for |ε| ≳ 0.5 the
  system is monostable *polar*, and sign(ε) selects which pole. One pole deepens as the
  other vanishes — "neither" does not occur.

A Boolean lattice 2² has a single complement type; hosting a neutralisation path class
and a polar-selection path class as distinct moves is Petitot's obstruction, realised here
as two path classes in the cusp plane *from the same algebraic jet*. This is an
operational witness in the cusp plane, not a topos-theoretic theorem and not D3. A
norm-only control (b ≡ τ) cannot flip wells: direction is load-bearing, not cosmetic.

### 4.3 The field of seven squares (R4_GREEN)

The octonion's non-Booleanisability is a property of the *field* of squares, not of one

## 5. Functor F

**Definition 5.1 (Functor F, working form).** For a jet object (L⋆, u, τ, ε) — a Fano

stratification invariant of V(·; a(ε), b(ε)) under Φ_fp: the number of wells (1 or 2 in

{x : V(x) ≤ c} at the canonical regular value. F is jet-functorial: the odd associator
jet controls the first-order topological deformation (db/dε|₀ = ±1).

### 5.1 Uniformity (G_GREEN)

Across all 7 basis-aligned Fano lines and all off-line units, the jet is single-axis with
‖α‖ = 2, and Φ_fp, the path classes, and the Betti behaviour are uniform
(`functor_f_g2_covariance_contract.py`, 6/6 clauses). F does not depend on the choice of
line.

### 5.2 The covariance obstruction and its repair (H_CHARACTERISED, E_GREEN)

Reading the polar coordinate b off the jet by coordinate argmax is **not** G₂-covariant:
under generic sampled automorphisms of 𝕆, the argmax axis moves in 100% of 200 samples,
and under a continuous one-parameter subgroup exp(t·𝔤₂) the naive path can flip poles
mid-orbit. The mechanism is located: argmax is a coordinate object, not a natural pairing.
The constructive repair is the pairing

  b_cov = ⟨α, e_m⟩,

which is G₂-invariant to 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁵ over the same samples, and invariant along the
continuous orbit; up to scalar multiple it is the unique G₂-invariant linear functional on
the single-axis jet, since the jet spans a 1-dimensional subspace and e_m is the only
direction distinguished by the (line, off-line) configuration. With b_cov as the canonical
polar coordinate, the entire ladder is G₂-equivariant (E_GREEN, 4/4).

### 5.3 Functoriality on the field of couplings (K_CHARACTERISED)

Extend F to cross-line couplings. F is strictly additive exactly on couplings that stay
inside an associative (Fano) sub-algebra — 14 of 42 configurations; on the remaining 28 it
picks up a defect, and the defect is exactly the cross-associator: order-1, G₂-covariant,
‖α‖ = 2. This is a characterisation of F's functoriality (additive iff associative, with
one controlled covariant correction), not a refutation of it.

### 5.4 Coherence and closure of the algebraic tower (P_GREEN, Q_GREEN)

The correction of §5.3 is the G₂ 3-form φ under its defining contraction identity.
Verified exactly on the octonion core (all 7⁴ tuples, worst deviation 0.0):

  φ·φ = δδ − δδ − ψ   (the standard G₂ contraction identity; Bryant, Karigiannis),

and every pairwise contraction of {φ, ψ} returns a member of {δ, φ, ψ} — four exact
identities with integer coefficients 24, 4, −2, −4. The tower of algebraic rupture
invariants therefore **closes under contraction at order 2**: the octonion core generates

under contraction. (The statement is scoped to contraction within the octonion core; it

to larger algebras.) This is a boundary theorem for the programme:
where the algebraic sensor saturates, further reach (to curvature- or bifurcation-type
orders) requires a functor, not further contraction — which is what §§5.1–5.3 supply.

## 6. Cross-column probes: two located obstructions, one located attachment, one positive thread

### 6.1 The Petitot bridge stops at the cusp (B_OBSTRUCTED, PHI_JETS_VANISH_PROVEN)

The cusp has two canonical G₂-invariant controls — depth (even jet) and tilt (odd jet) —
and closes canonically (§4). The butterfly potential, Petitot's vehicle for the
complex/neutral term, needs a third control; the natural candidate is the antisymmetric
cubic invariant φ(α₁, α₂, α₃) of three coupling jets.

**Theorem 6.1 (the jets carry no cubic invariant).** φ(a₁, a₂, a₃) = 0 for every triple of
single-axis associator jets.

*Proof sketch.* Each jet is single-axis with magnitude exactly 2 (Lemma 4.1), so φ on jets
reduces to φ on triples of basis axes. By PG(2,2) incidence, no Fano line lies in the
complement of a 4-point support, and direct check over all 168 Fano-plane configurations
gives 0 axis-triples lying on a line; by trilinearity and alternation of φ, every such
evaluation vanishes. The vanishing was first measured over 840 configurations, then
upgraded to this structural argument; two independent adversarial reimplementations
(recursive doubling under a conjugate convention; exact rational arithmetic under an
unrelated explicit sign table) reproduced it exactly, and confirmed it is non-vacuous:
generic non-jet associator triples do give φ ≠ 0. Contract:
`functor_f_phi_jets_vanish_contract.py` (PHI_JETS_VANISH_PROVEN, 6/6). ∎

**Reading.** The algebra → morphodynamics bridge reaches the cusp canonically and the
butterfly not at all, for a located reason: the coupling jets are too rigid to carry a
third independent invariant. This rules out the *canonical* construction, not every
conceivable one (orientation-sensitive variants are a named open edge) — and it sharpens
the documented Petitot divergence: the obstruction sits at the butterfly, not the cusp.

### 6.2 Ollivier–Ricci curvature is blind to the algebra (M_CHARACTERISED)

On the canonical Fano-incidence graphs (K₇; the Heawood graph), Ollivier–Ricci and

distinct signed octonion multiplication tables — 16 distinct φ — induce the identical

associator (order 1) are parallel sensors; no single scalar unifies them.

### 6.3 The order-3 attachment (SECONDARY_TERNARY_LOCATED, NO_CANONICAL_FILL)

Define the secondary ternary operation where the primary associator vanishes (Massey's

a Massey representative. An initial (a·c)·b candidate "canonical invariant" was refuted by
a bracketing-consistency test and is recorded as a near-miss, not claimed. A
symmetry-canonical fill at the single-fibre level hits a structural wall: the stabiliser
of a single ZD under the fibre action is trivial (the line-stabiliser S₄ permutes the six
fibres of a support-class rather than fixing one), so there is no group to average over;

## 7. The exceptional frontier: the ADE–Wildgen analysis

### 7.1 The concrete bridge: φ inside the E₆ cubic

Let N be the cubic form (determinant) of the Albert algebra J₃(𝕆) — the F₄-invariant that

  N(a, x; b, y; c, z) = abc − a·n(x) − b·n(y) − c·n(z) + 2 Re(xyz).

**Theorem 7.1 (G; φ is the E₆ cubic cross-term).**
(i) Re[x,y,z] = 0 and Re((xy)z) = Re(x(yz)): the real part of a length-3 word is a
well-defined, bracketing-independent trilinear form on 𝕆.
(ii) N is G₂-invariant (relative deviation 3 × 10⁻¹⁵), realising G₂ ⊂ F₄ = Aut(J₃(𝕆)).
(iii) For imaginary x, y, z ∈ 𝕆, **Re(x·y·z) = −φ(x,y,z)** (deviation 4 × 10⁻¹⁵): the G₂
3-form at the centre of the Functor-F programme *is* the imaginary restriction of the
octonion cross-term of the E₆/Albert cubic invariant.
(iv) The vector-valued associator [x,y,z] is a separate object (the ψ/4-form side): φ is
scalar, the associator is not, and an earlier conflation of the two was located and
retracted in the programme's own ledger.
(v) The bridge is cubic-specific: Re of a word is bracketing-independent at length ≤ 3
(5 × 10⁻¹⁵) and not at length ≥ 4 (spread ~70); the mechanism does not lift to the E₇
quartic.

### 7.2 E₇ and E₈: where the clean phenomenon ends

The Freudenthal quartic on the E₇ side (the honest degree-4 exceptional invariant, built
via the trace/adjoint rather than Re-of-a-word) was constructed and verified; it sees the

magic square

  𝔢₈ = der(𝕆) ⊕ (Im 𝕆 ⊗ J₃(𝕆)₀) ⊕ der(J₃(𝕆)) = 14 + 7·26 + 52 = 248,

so the whole tower G₂(14) ⊂ F₄(52) ⊂ E₆(78) ⊂ E₇(133) ⊂ E₈(248) is built from 𝕆 and
Jordan algebras over 𝕆, whose entire G₂-invariant form-content is {g, φ} (with ψ = ∗φ,

### 7.3 The ADE label-match (a fact, not a finding)

Arnold's exceptional *singularities* E₆, E₇, E₈ share their Dynkin labels with the
exceptional *Lie algebras* via the McKay correspondence; the octonions generate the
exceptional Lie groups via the Freudenthal–Tits magic square. Both are standard
mathematics, cited here to place the programme. The programme itself is complete at the
base of the tower: every executable rung is G₂-representation-theoretic (the forms φ, ψ;
the symmetry Aut(𝕆) = G₂ and its Fano subgroup PSL(2,7); the ZD geometry acted on by
diagonal G₂/PSL(2,7)), and §§7.1–7.2 are the *only* rungs that touch the exceptional
groups, concretely, through the cubic and the magic-square dimension count. No rung
constructs F₄, E₆, E₇, or E₈.

### 7.4 The Wildgen conjecture, quarantined

Wildgen's catastrophe-theoretic semantics argues that rich (four-actant) semantic
morphology requires the exceptional singularities E₆, E₇, E₈ (and X₉). Combined with §7.3
this suggests the conjecture that *the organising centre of rich semantic morphology is
the octonionic exceptional structure* — that Petitot/Wildgen's singularity-theoretic
semantics and the Cayley–Dickson algebra are two faces of one exceptional geometry.

**We state this as a conjecture, and we do not claim it.** The label-match is real; the
semantic realisation is conjectural. The landmine is named in the programme's own
boundary note: analogy mistaken for theorem — the "catastrophe controversy" that damaged
the first wave of applied catastrophe theory. The honest entry point, if the conjecture is
ever pursued, is the first place a functor-F object would have to leave G₂: a φ/ψ/ZD
structure that is F₄-natural but not G₂-natural (e.g. on J₃(𝕆) rather than 𝕆). Theorem
7.1 is a step of exactly that kind — φ sitting inside the Albert cubic — and Theorem 7.2's
cubic-specificity is the boundary of that step. Nothing further is asserted.

## 10. Discussion and open problems

**Where the programme stands.** The algebraic column is closed: φ and ψ are the only
algebraic rupture invariants the octonion core generates under contraction (§5.4), the
bridge from them to cusp stratification is functorial and G₂-equivariant (§§4–5), the
cross-column frontier is mapped — two coincidence-obstructions (butterfly, curvature) and
one genuine attachment (order-3 to order-2) — and the symmetry underlying all three
orders is one Fano plane with its full PSL(2,7) (§6.4). The exceptional placement of φ is
concrete at E₆ and honestly capped at E₈ (§7). The frontier is not uniformly negative,
and it is not an identity theorem: it is a map of exactly where the correspondence holds,
where it stops, and why.

**Open problems.**
1. Prove the growth/fibre laws of §3.4 inductively for all b ≥ 4 (or falsify at level 7:
   Z(7) = 13 884 predicted).
2. A symmetry-canonical fill of the order-3 quotient at the level of the 6-fibre
   support-class, as an S₄- or PSL(2,7)-module (§6.3); alternatively, impose an explicit
   A∞/differential structure and compute the genuine Massey representative.
3. Orientation-sensitive (signed) variants of the incidence-graph curvature probe (§6.2);
   prove edge-transitivity forces constant Ollivier–Ricci curvature from Aut(K₇) = S₇.
4. A variety-level correspondence between a *derived* (not unit-chosen) semantic
   potential and an algebraic locus — the only honest route to reopening anything like
   D3; not expected soon, and not claimed.
5. The F₄-natural-but-not-G₂-natural object named in §7.4, if the exceptional-semantic
   conjecture is ever to be more than an analogy.

**Limitations.** Theorem 3.3's analytic steps are verified numerically and in exact
arithmetic at the stated clauses, not formalised in a proof assistant. G₂-equivariance is
constructive over sampled generic and continuous automorphisms, not an enumeration of the
group. The census laws are exact at three levels and inductive beyond. The semantic
inputs A₀, τ of Φ_fp are declared inputs, not derived quantities.
