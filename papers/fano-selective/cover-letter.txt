Dear Editors of the Journal of Automated Reasoning,

We submit the enclosed manuscript, "Fano-Selective Reassociation:
Formal Verification of Cocycle Coherence for Non-Associative Compiler
Rewrites," for consideration as an original research article.

The manuscript addresses a question at the intersection of automated
reasoning and compiler correctness: when is it safe for an optimiser
to reassociate expressions in a non-associative algebra? The octonions
-- the largest normed division algebra -- are non-associative, yet
not uniformly so: exactly 175 of 343 ordered triples of imaginary
basis elements are associative, and 168 are not. The boundary is
determined by the Fano plane PG(2,2).

Our contributions are:

1. A two-layer algebraic structure (Definition 3.1, Theorem 3.7)
   separating cocycle-coherent reassociation at the algebraic level
   from semantics-preserving rewrites at the compiler level. The
   pentagon coherence axiom is verified for all 4,096 quadruples
   over the octonion basis via the 3-cocycle condition of
   Albuquerque-Majid.

2. A complete Lean 4 formalisation: 122 theorems across four files,
   with no uses of sorry and no Mathlib dependency. All counting
   results (175 + 168 = 343, the 84/84 dagger decomposition, the
   pentagon condition) are proved by native_decide over finite
   domains. The total build time is under 3 seconds.

3. An implementation in Sounio, a self-hosted programming language
   whose compiler uses the Fano predicate as an e-graph
   reassociation guard. The NonAssoc algebraic effect tracks
   non-associative multiplications at the type level. The compiler
   reaches a bit-identical fixed point (gen2 = gen3) and is
   available as a public artifact.

We believe this work fits the scope of the Journal of Automated
Reasoning because the central contribution is a formally verified
decidability result: every associator property of the Cayley-Dickson
octonions is decidable over a finite domain and provable by
native_decide without external libraries. The methodology --
exhaustive enumeration plus native code execution during Lean
elaboration -- may be of independent interest for verifying
combinatorial algebraic identities in proof assistants.

The manuscript has not been submitted elsewhere. All Lean source
files, Sounio compiler source, and runtime verification examples are
publicly available at https://github.com/Sounio-lang/sounio (tag
v1.0.0-rc1).

Thank you for your consideration.
