SUPPORTED CLAIM
An unacknowledged directed request crossed a real Kubernetes node transition
from t560-proxmox to r740-proxmox and remained eligible for replay through a
third distinct successor generation. After its durable ACK, a fourth generation
suppressed injection. Every admitted generation carried an Ed25519-signed
receipt over the canonical native-Sounio policy facts, adapter digest, verdict,
and predecessor receipt token. Signed promotion requires a private Sounio proof
type that is distinct from unsigned promotion. The four retained receipts were
independently verified after the Pods, PVC, and signing Secret had been
destroyed, using only the retained public key and the native Sounio adapter.
The signature establishes integrity and authorship under the ephemeral workload
key; it does not establish correctness against a faulty keyholder.

RESEARCH HYPOTHESIS
The candidate contribution is proof-carrying resurrection: a crash successor
is not promoted merely because storage survived, but because a private,
checker-enforced signed-predecessor proof term is supplied to a distinct
language-level typestate transition. The separate ACK control distinguishes
proof-gated admission from unconditional inbox replay. Whether the signed
certificate denotes a correct predecessor decision remains a trust-boundary
question, not a consequence of Ed25519.

BOUNDARIES
This is one four-generation, two-node compute relocation over one shared Ceph
RBD PVC and one ephemeral signer co-located with the workload. The state did not
replicate or migrate between independent stores, RWOP prevented simultaneous
writers, and no partition or consensus case was exercised. A holder of the
private key can sign policy-acceptable but semantically false facts; the probe
confirmed that the standalone verifier and signed typestate admitted such a
certificate. An independent canary commitment noticed the changed receipt, but
only after the successor had spawned. This establishes neither production
key custody or rotation, threshold or Byzantine signing, multi-replica
consensus, exactly-once external effects, loss of the retained PVC, Madaros
linear-checker parity, a formal failure model, a PL/CS novelty result, nor
research priority. The hypothesis still requires a formal semantics, repeated
fault campaigns, and direct comparison against actor resurrection, durable
workflows, fencing tokens, proof-carrying systems, and crash-consistent logs.
